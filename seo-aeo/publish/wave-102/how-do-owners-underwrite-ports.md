# How do owners underwrite ports?

Underwriting a port is a regime-and-income-mix job, not a second infrastructure-as-an-asset-class page. UNCTADstat (data updated 15 April 2026) prints world container port throughput of 920 million TEU in 2024, up 6.9 percent, after 0.6 percent in each of 2022 and 2023. The decade average is just over 3 percent a year; since 2014 the world has added 37 percent, or 248 million TEU. UNCTAD’s Review of Maritime Transport 2025 (24 September 2025) says the landlord model is predominant among Port Performance Scorecard ports — over 60 percent — while 45 to 50 percent of income still comes from direct port dues and concession and property income was 24 percent in 2024. This page is not a ranking and not valuation advice.

## Throughput, then the landlord file

Official [UNCTADstat container throughput](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/245) (data updated **15 April 2026**) and UNCTAD’s [*Review of Maritime Transport 2025*](https://unctad.org/publication/review-maritime-transport-2025) (24 September 2025), [Chapter 4](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/rmt2025ch4_en.pdf), are the sourced underwriting print:

1. World container port throughput was **920 million TEU** in **2024**, **+6.9 percent**. Throughput rose **0.6 percent** in each of **2022** and **2023**. Over the last decade, volumes increased just over **3 percent** a year. Since **2014**, the world has added **37 percent**, or **248 million TEU**.
2. The **landlord** model — port authorities manage the infrastructure; private operators run the operations — is the predominant operating approach among ports on UNCTAD’s Port Performance Scorecard. **Over 60 percent** are structured that way.
3. **45–50 percent** of income still comes from **direct port dues** (charges on vessels and cargo). Concession and property-related income was **24 percent** in **2024**. That mix is the cash-flow file. It is not a listed-infra multiple.
