# Royal family investment offices — how court-adjacent capital actually sits

A royal family investment office is a long-horizon owner that sits beside a court, a ruling house, or a royal holding company. It can look like a family office, a sovereign wealth fund, or both — and the legal line matters. Some royal capital is private family wealth. Some is state capital with a royal shareholder. Universal Asset Owners treats these desks as owners, not as celebrity balance sheets. This is not a list of net worths and not investment advice.

## The distinction that keeps journalists out of trouble

**Private royal family office.** The principal is a family. The office looks like other multi-generational family offices: confidentiality, succession, direct deals, a CIO who can be fired at dinner. See [What is a family office?](/what-is-a-family-office/).

**State capital with a royal context.** A sovereign or strategic fund may be chaired or overseen in a system where the ruling house and the state are close. That does not make the fund “the king's checkbook.” It makes governance, disclosure and politics part of the investment problem. Start with the [Santiago Principles](https://www.ifswf.org/santiago-principles) and our [sovereign wealth fund](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) explainer.

If you cannot say which of those two you are looking at, you are not ready to publish a number.

## What we will and will not write

We will write: mandate, official governance, public holdings, named CIOs when the institution names them, office cities, and how peers should read a deal.

We will not write: unsourced “richest royal” lists, leaked family trees, or gossip that cannot survive a correction.

## Where these desks sit (public geography, not a leak)

Public reporting and official sites already place important royal-adjacent and state-royal capital in the Gulf, parts of Europe, and Asia. Use the institution's own site. City pages ([Riyadh](/careers/cities/riyadh/), [Abu Dhabi](/careers/cities/abu-dhabi/), [London](/careers/cities/london/), [Singapore](/careers/cities/singapore/)) exist for people who move to work *at* those desks.

## For CIOs and staff

The job is still asset-owner work: residual risk, a horizon, and a principal who is not a public pension board. Confidentiality is stricter. Public mistakes are louder. Read the [relocation playbook](/careers/intelligence/relocating-for-an-asset-owner-role/) before you treat an offer like a bank posting.
