**The Government Pension Fund of Thailand is a 1996 statutory savings scheme — not a 1979 defined-benefit pile, and not a US-dollar AUM story.**

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

This page overwrites a live UAO stub that printed **฿1.8 trillion (USD 50+ billion)**, a **1979** founding date, a defined-benefit story, homemade member counts, and a 4–5 percent CAGR. Those lines were not taken from GPF’s English pages.

Print the baht. Do not invent a US-dollar AUM from a homemade FX rate.

## What the two NAV lines mean

GPF’s own English table prints two NAVs because the scheme has a **reserves account** beside member savings. The larger figure (THB 1,575,529,445,951.16) includes that reserves line. The smaller figure (THB 611,583,713,730.69) excludes it. A writer who picks one number and dollarizes it is not quoting the fund.

The member account is a **savings** layer. The state still pays a separate gratuity or pension. Calling GPF a single defined-benefit AUM number is the wrong legal story.

## What is not official

- A **1979** founding date. The Act is B.E. 2539 / AD 1996.
- **USD 50+ billion**, or any other homemade dollar AUM.
- A single **฿1.8 trillion** figure that does not match either official NAV line.
- Homemade **1.2 million / 600,000** member counts, or a **4–5 percent CAGR**, until GPF prints them on a dated English page.
- Treating GPF as a government agency or state enterprise.
