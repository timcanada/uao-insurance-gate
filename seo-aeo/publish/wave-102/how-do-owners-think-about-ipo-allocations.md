# How do owners think about IPO allocations?

An IPO allocation for an asset owner is a new-issue rule, not a hedge-fund retail pitch. FINRA Rule 5130(a) bars a member from selling a new issue to an account in which a restricted person has a beneficial interest. Restricted persons include broker-dealers and their personnel, finders and fiduciaries, and portfolio managers — anyone with authority to buy or sell securities for a bank, savings-and-loan, insurer, investment company, investment adviser, or collective account — plus certain immediate family. Rule 5130(c) then carves out the owner accounts that matter: a registered investment company; an ERISA plan qualified under Internal Revenue Code 401(a) that is not sponsored solely by a broker-dealer; a large employee retirement plan with at least 10,000 participants and $10 billion in assets; a state or municipal government benefits plan; and a 501(c)(3). Rule 5131(b) spinning does not apply to those same 5130(c) exemptions. This page is not a ranking and not allocation advice.

## Restricted person, then the owner exemptions

Official [FINRA Rule 5130](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5130) and [FINRA Rule 5131](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5131) are the sourced mechanism:

1. **5130(a).** A member may not sell a new issue to an account in which a **restricted person** has a beneficial interest. Restricted persons include broker-dealers and personnel, finders and fiduciaries acting for the managing underwriter, and **portfolio managers** — anyone with authority to buy or sell securities for a bank, savings-and-loan, insurer, investment company, investment adviser, or collective investment account — plus certain immediate family.
2. **5130(c) exemptions that matter to owners.** The ban does not apply to (1) a registered investment-company account; (7) an **ERISA** plan qualified under Internal Revenue Code **401(a)** that is not sponsored solely by a broker-dealer; (8) an employee retirement-benefit plan with at least **10,000** participants **and $10 billion** in assets, directed by a fiduciary, and not designed to evade the rule; (9) a **state or municipal government** benefits plan; and (10) a **501(c)(3)** charitable organization.
3. **5131(b) spinning.** A member may not allocate a new issue to an account in which an executive officer or director of a public company, or of a covered non-public company — or a person they materially support — has a beneficial interest, if the issuer is a current, recent, or expected investment-banking client (the past **12 months**, the next **3 months**, or an implied future mandate). **5131(b)(2)** says that spinning ban **does not apply** to accounts described in **5130(c)(1)–(3) and (5)–(11)**. The government-plan, 501(c)(3), and large-retirement exemptions **carry through**.

A [fund of one](/what-is-a-fund-of-one/) is the wrapper. This page is the **new-issue eligibility** of the beneficial owners inside that wrapper. Do not invent how many shares a pension “usually” gets.
