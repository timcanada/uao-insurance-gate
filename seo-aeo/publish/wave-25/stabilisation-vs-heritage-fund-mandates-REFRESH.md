# Stabilisation vs heritage mandates: Chile ESSF is US$3.88 billion, not $10.8 billion

The mandate contrast is real. The live dollars are not. Chile’s Ministry of Finance put the Economic and Social Stabilization Fund at **US$3,884.04 million** at the end of **June 2026**. The live page, dated 7 July 2026, still says the ESSF held **approximately $10.8 billion by 2023**, was launched in **2006** with **$4.5 billion**, and then assigns Future Fund **A$219.3 billion (2024)** and GPFG **approximately $1.3 trillion AUD**.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

Hacienda’s own table shows withdrawals already exceed contributions. That is what a stabilisation reserve does. Anyone still quoting **$10.8 billion** is teaching a pre-drawdown stock. Do **not** treat the Future Fund homepage **$356.0bn** as Future Fund AUM. That widget is labelled **total funds under management**. The live UAO institution page already has the sleeve at **A$269.1 billion** at 31 March 2026. Do not overwrite that URL this wake.

Print GPFG in **kroner**. Do not invent an Australian-dollar equivalent.

## What the mandates are

Stabilisation funds exist to smooth a budget when commodity or cycle revenue falls. Heritage / savings funds exist to compound a corpus for later generations. Chile’s ESSF is the first job. [GPFG](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) and Australia’s [Future Fund](/future-fund-australia-explained/) are closer to the second. Do not file [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) as a sovereign heritage fund because the live page needed a US peer.

- GPFG **$1.3 trillion AUD**.
- CalPERS **$440 billion** as a heritage-SWF exhibit.
