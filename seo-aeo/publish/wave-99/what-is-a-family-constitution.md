# What is a family constitution?

A family constitution is a private governance paper — who decides, who inherits a vote, and what the investment committee may do. It is not the SEC family-office exemption. That exemption, 17 CFR § 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1, tests clients, ownership, control, and whether the office holds itself out to the public as an adviser. A constitution does not create that exclusion. ## A constitution is not the exemption

The Advisers Act family-office rule is a **statutory exclusion**, not a family meeting note. [17 CFR § 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.202(a)(11)(G)-1) (76 FR 37994, **29 June 2011**) says a family office shall not be considered an investment adviser if it is a company that:

1. Has **no clients other than family clients** — with a one-year grace if a non-family person becomes a client by death or other involuntary transfer.
2. Is **wholly owned by family clients** and **exclusively controlled** (directly or indirectly) by one or more family members and/or family entities.
3. **Does not hold itself out to the public** as an investment adviser.

The same section defines **family member** as all lineal descendants of a common ancestor — including by adoption, stepchildren, foster children, and a minor for whom a family member became legal guardian — and those descendants’ spouses or spousal equivalents, provided the common ancestor is **no more than 10 generations** removed from the youngest generation of family members.

A constitution can name the next generation, the voting threshold, and the investment-committee chair. None of those sentences is the three-part test. If the office takes a non-family client, is owned outside the family, or holds itself out as an adviser, the paper does not save the exclusion.

## What an owner is actually buying

A multi-generational office that writes a constitution is buying a **decision rule** the next generation can read — not a second family-office logo.

The SEC test is whether the company is a family office under the rule. The constitution is whether the family can still decide after a death, a divorce, or a hire.
