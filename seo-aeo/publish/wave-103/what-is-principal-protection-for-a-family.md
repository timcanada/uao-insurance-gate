# What is principal protection for a family?

Principal protection on a structured note is an issuer promise, not a family-office spending rule and not a foundation 5 percent test. The joint SEC and FINRA investor bulletin (2 June 2011) says products labelled principal protection, capital guarantee, absolute return, or minimum return are not risk-free. Any promise to repay some or all of the money invested depends on the creditworthiness of the issuer. Protection typically applies only if the note is held to maturity, and some products offer only partial or contingent protection. Structured notes are unsecured promises to pay; they do not represent ownership of a portfolio. This page is not a ranking and not investment advice.

## A label, an issuer, a maturity date

The official [SEC/FINRA bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/structured) (**2 June 2011**) and FINRA’s [structured-notes explainer](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/structured-notes-principal-protection) are the sourced definition:

1. A structured note with principal protection **combines a bond with a derivative**. It typically pairs a zero-coupon bond with an option or other derivative whose payoff is linked to an underlying asset, index, or benchmark. Structured products **do not represent ownership** of a portfolio of assets. They are **promises to pay** made by the issuer.
2. Any promise to repay some or all of the money invested **depends on the creditworthiness of the issuer**. If the issuer goes bankrupt, holders are **unsecured creditors** and may recover little or nothing. The bulletin names structured notes with principal protection issued by **Lehman Brothers Holdings**. Protection typically applies **only if the note is held to maturity**. Some products return **100 percent** of principal; others return as little as **10 percent**. Contingent protection may provide **no** protection at all.
3. Most structured notes **do not** offer principal protection. Where they do, the return above any principal guarantee is a formula — participation rate, high-water mark, accrual, or a capped “shark fin.” The bulletin says you typically receive the protection from the issuer **only at maturity**. Early sale can return **substantially less** than the purchase price.

Mixing a private foundation and the family-office book is a [4941 self-dealing](/how-do-family-offices-think-about-philanthropy-vs-the-book/) problem. This page is the **note**. Do not flatten “protect the principal” into a spending slogan.

## What an owner is actually buying

A family office buying a “principal-protected” sleeve is buying **issuer credit and a formula**, not FDIC insurance and not a 501(c)(3) distribution test.
