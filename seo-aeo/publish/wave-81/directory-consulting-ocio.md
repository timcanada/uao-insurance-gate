# Consulting and OCIO for sovereign funds, pensions and family offices

Asset owners buy consulting and OCIO for work a board or a family can defend: asset allocation, manager selection, implementation, and an outsourced CIO office when the desk is too small to staff every sleeve. This hub is Universal Asset Owners' map of that category — written for the desk that hires, and usable by the firms that want to be found. It is not a ranking and not investment advice.

The job is already defined on [What is an OCIO?](/what-is-an-ocio/). **Do not treat this directory page as a second definition.** A listing here is not an endorsement. Featured placements are marked paid.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## What owners are actually buying

A public pension that hires a general consultant is buying a process the board can minute. The question is often fiduciary: what was considered, who decided, what was recorded — see [fiduciary duty](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/).

A family office that hires an OCIO is buying a CIO office it does not want to staff. The principal still owns the policy. The firm owns the implementation.

A sovereign or strategic fund that hires a specialist consultant is buying a sleeve, a market-entry memo, or a governance review — not a retail model portfolio.

If you sell into this room, say which of those jobs you do. If you buy, ask for the last three mandates *like yours*, redacted.

## The questions a CIO or board should ask

1. Who is the working partner, and have they sat on your side of the investment committee?
2. Where does advice end and discretion begin?
3. How do you handle a conflict when you also run a manager or an OCIO product?
4. What will you refuse to put in the board pack?
