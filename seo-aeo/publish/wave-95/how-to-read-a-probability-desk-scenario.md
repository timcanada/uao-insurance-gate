# How to read a Probability Desk scenario

A Probability Desk scenario is a named outcome over a named window, with a probability and the evidence that informs it — not a forecast and not a trade idea. Universal Asset Owners’ [methodology](/methodology/) says the Desk re-scores when new evidence lands. Full-simulation reports are flagship products and disclose their model. Nothing on the Desk is investment advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second Probability Desk overwrite.** Do not mint `/probability-desk-explained/`, `/how-to-read-a-scenario/`, or `/uao-probability-desk/`. Do not reprint a leftover house AUM to decorate a weight.

## What the methodology already requires

[Methodology](/methodology/) (version 1.0, **28 May 2026**) is the product file:

- The Probability Desk is the **afternoon scenario product**
- Each scenario is a **discrete, named outcome** over a **stated time window**
- It **shows the evidence** that informs each probability
- It **re-scores when new evidence lands**
- Probability ranges use a structured method: a **base rate** from the historical record where one exists, plus a small number of stated up- and down-weights with their sources. Where a probability is shown without a stated base rate, the post says so
- A separate review pass checks recency drift, dated framing, source quality, and overreach
- **What we do not do:** publish a probability without showing what informs it; run a Monte Carlo on a free post and present the output as the work; present probabilities as forecasts of the future. They are the platform’s reading of the evidence at a **stated moment**

Inputs named on that page: public reporting, official statements, treaty and legislative text, primary economic data, satellite and shipping data where available and licensed, expert work cited by name, and UAO’s own market and capital-flow tracking.

## A live example (so the form is not abstract)

The live [Probability Desk](/probability-desk/) index, checked **26 August 2026**, led with the **22 August 2026** edition: “Does Brent print $100 by 30 September?” The Desk put **58%** on a $100 print within 30 trading days from spot **$93.26**, and said that reading sat **below its own 50,000-path model**. Those are the numbers on that edition — not a forecast we will update on this explainer.

Read the date first. Then the named outcome. Then the window. Then the evidence. Then the weight. If any of those four is missing, do not put the number in a board pack.

A [tripwire](/what-is-a-tripwire-in-scenario-analysis/) is the evidence that would move that weight. It is a different job.
