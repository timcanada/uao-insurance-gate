# Custody, administration and operations for long-horizon owners

Asset owners buy custody and administration for a number the board, the auditor, and the beneficiary file can all use: safekeeping, settlement, look-through, NAV, and the unglamorous work of making private-markets capital calls land on time. This hub is Universal Asset Owners' map of that category. It is not a ranking and not operational advice.

A listing here is not an endorsement. Featured placements are marked paid.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## What owners are actually buying

A public pension buys a custodian that can survive a records request and a legislative hearing. The failure mode is a number that does not match the board pack.

A sovereign or strategic fund buys cross-border safekeeping, sanctions screening, and a book that can be shown to a ministry.

A family office buys confidentiality and the ability to see public and private holdings in one place without putting the family file in a founder Slack.

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) are not asset managers. The custodian works for the owner. That is a [fiduciary](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/) fact, not a sales line.

If you sell into this room, say which of those jobs you do. If you buy, ask who owns the data when you leave.

## The questions a CIO or operations head should ask

1. Who is the working client lead, and have they run an owner book — not only a manager book?
2. How do you reconcile private-markets NAV to the custodian cash account?
3. Where is the data stored, who can see the beneficiary or family file, and what happens on exit?
4. What will you refuse to put in an SLA?
