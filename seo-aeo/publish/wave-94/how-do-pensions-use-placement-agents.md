# How pensions use placement agents

A public pension uses a placement agent as a disclosed intermediary on someone else’s raise — a finder the board can minute — not as a substitute for diligence and not as a co-invest sleeve. California’s CalPERS regulation already defines the job: a compensated finder, solicitor, marketer, consultant, broker, or other intermediary hired for the manager’s benefit. Universal Asset Owners maps the category on the [placement-agent directory](/directory/placement-agents/). This page is not fundraising advice.

## What a regulated pension file already requires

Cal. Code Regs. Tit. 2, § 559 (filed **28 January 2011**; operative the same day) is the public CalPERS rule. Three facts from that text, not from a pitch book:

1. A **Placement Agent** is a person hired, engaged, or retained for the benefit of an external manager or an investment fund, who acts **for compensation** as a finder, solicitor, marketer, consultant, broker, or other intermediary in connection with an offer or sale to CalPERS.
2. The external manager must deliver a **Placement Agent Information Disclosure** to CalPERS staff within **45 days** of the time investment discussions are initiated, and in any event **before diligence is finished**. The file includes the agent’s name, résumé, the written agreement, and **the nature, timing, and value of all compensation**.
3. For new contracts and amendments, CalPERS staff must **decline** the manager if the disclosure shows an agent who is **not registered** with the SEC, FINRA, or — where appropriate — the CFTC (non-U.S. agents covering non-U.S. CalPERS vehicles may show a recognised home-country registration instead). The manager, not the plan, pays the agent. Staff compile a **monthly public report** of names and agreed compensation.

Employees of the manager who spend **one-third or more** of the calendar year managing the manager’s own assets are carved out of that definition. That is the in-house IR exception, not a licence to hide a hired finder.

A Gulf strategic fund or a family office may run a quieter file. The question does not change: who pays the intermediary, and can the investment committee minute it?

## What the pension is *not* buying

Sitting alongside a GP on a deal is [co-investment](/what-is-co-investment-in-private-equity/). A secondaries book is a different door. An investment bank on a corporate action is a third. Do not collapse those jobs onto this slug.

The four questions that belong in the IC pre-read:

1. Who is the working partner, and have they sat on *this* side of a committee?
2. Who pays them, and where does that sit in the GP’s fee stack?
3. How do they handle a conflict when they also cover a competitor or a secondaries buyer?
4. What will they refuse to put in the data-room memo?
