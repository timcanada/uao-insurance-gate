# Internal vs external CIO office — what the board is actually buying

A board can staff a CIO office or rent one. The assets stay the owner’s either way. This Careers Intelligence briefing sits beside Universal Asset Owners' [OCIO definition](/what-is-an-ocio/) and the [consulting and OCIO directory](/directory/consulting-ocio/). It is not a vendor shortlist and not investment advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second OCIO definition.** Do not 301 it onto `/what-is-an-ocio/` or `/directory/consulting-ocio/`. Do not invent an OCIO-market AUM to decorate a staffing memo.

## The choice is a fiduciary file

An [OCIO](/what-is-an-ocio/) is a firm that manages an institution’s portfolio on a delegated basis — strategy, managers, implementation — inside a policy the board still owns. [Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) hold capital for their own account. Renting the office does not rent the [fiduciary duty](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/).

Internal is a payroll, a committee calendar, and a hiring problem. External is a contract, a conflict file, and an exit clause. Mid-sized pensions, endowments, and family offices hit this choice when the book is too complex for a part-time committee and too small to staff every sleeve.

If you cannot say where advice ends and discretion begins, you do not have a model. You have a meeting.

## What to pressure-test before you choose

1. **Decision rights.** Who signs the ticket, and who minutes the exception?
2. **Conflicts.** Does the firm also run a manager, a fund, or another owner in your peer set?
3. **People.** Who is the working partner, and have they sat on your side of the table?
4. **Exit.** Who owns the data, the models, and the manager relationships when you leave?
5. **The file.** What will you refuse to put in the board pack?
