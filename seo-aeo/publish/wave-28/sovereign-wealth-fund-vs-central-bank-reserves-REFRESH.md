# SWF vs central-bank reserves: no official $15 trillion pile — and ADSWF is not a filing

A sovereign wealth fund and a central-bank reserve book are different legal jobs. That contrast is real. The live dollar table is not. The live page still catalogues **over 1,000 SWFs** with **combined assets exceeding $15 trillion**, assigns GPFG **$1.4 trillion**, ADIA **$170 billion**, an invented **Abu Dhabi Sovereign Wealth Fund (ADSWF) $174 billion**, ICD **$183 billion**, KIA **$712 billion**, PIF **$925 billion**, SAFE **$1.0–1.2 trillion**, and GIC **$688 billion**. Those are tracker numbers. They are not the filings.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

Print kroner for GPFG. Print dirhams for ICD. Do **not** invent a global SWF pile so reserves can be called “smaller.” IMF tracks official reserve assets; this desk did not fetch a current global reserve total this wake. Do not reprint the live **$7.2 trillion** mid-2024 line as if it were today’s IMF print.

There is no ADSWF filing. Abu Dhabi’s named books on this desk are [ADIA](/adia-abu-dhabi-investment-authority/), [Mubadala](/mubadala-investment-company/), and [ADQ](/adq-abu-dhabi/). Do not mint `/limad-explained/` (404).

## What the comparison can say

Reserves are held for monetary stability and must stay liquid. A [SWF](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) is a longer-horizon investment mandate, ring-fenced from day-to-day FX intervention. MAS official foreign reserves and GIC are **different legal persons** — that split is the Singapore exhibit, already corrected on [`/singapore-investment-landscape-gic-temasek-mas/`](/singapore-investment-landscape-gic-temasek-mas/). Norway’s [GPFG](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) invests **only abroad** under a Ministry of Finance mandate. That is not Norges Bank’s FX-reserve book.

A page that needs **1,000 SWFs / $15 trillion** and an invented **ADSWF** to make the contrast is a tracker page.
