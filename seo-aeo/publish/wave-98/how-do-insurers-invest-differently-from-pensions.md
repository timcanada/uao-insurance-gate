# How do insurers invest differently from pensions?

An insurer invests to pay policyholders under a capital rule. A pension fund invests to pay named members. In the EU, Solvency II is the prudential regime for insurance and reinsurance — in force January 2016 — and exists to protect policyholders and beneficiaries, not pension members. Those are different books. This page is not a ranking and not investment advice.

## What each book is already for

EIOPA’s official [Solvency II](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/browse/regulation-and-policy/solvency-ii_en) page is the insurer file. Three facts from that text:

1. Solvency II is the **prudential regime for insurance and reinsurance undertakings in the EU**. It **entered into force in January 2016**.
2. Its aim is the **adequate protection of policyholders and beneficiaries**.
3. It is built on **three pillars**: quantitative capital and valuation (I); governance, risk management, and the Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (II); supervisory reporting and public disclosure (III). It is **risk-based**: higher risk means a higher capital requirement for unexpected losses.

Directive 2009/138/EC Article 101 calibrates the Solvency Capital Requirement as a **99.5%** Value-at-Risk of basic own funds over **one year**. That is a one-year policyholder-protection number. It is not a pension funded-status ratio.

A [pension fund](/what-is-a-pension-fund/) is a pool invested to pay **named members**. Workers and employers contribute; benefits are paid in retirement. It is **not** a sovereign wealth fund even when the sponsor is a government. Japan’s GPIF remains the largest dedicated public pension at **¥293,643.7 billion** of investment assets at **31 March 2026**. Do not dollarize it so an insurer comparison can have a winner.

U.S. governmental plans sit outside ERISA Title I. That label already sits on [what an administrator is for a pension plan](/what-is-an-administrator-for-a-pension-plan/). Do not paste Solvency II onto CalPERS.
