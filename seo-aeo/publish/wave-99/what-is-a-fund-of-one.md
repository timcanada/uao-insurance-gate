# What is a fund-of-one?

A fund-of-one is a private fund with a single limited partner. It is still an issuer — a vehicle that would be an investment company but for Investment Company Act section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7). An SMA is a sleeve in the owner’s own name, not an issuer. One LP does not turn a fund into an account. This page is not a ranking and not operational advice.

## One LP is still a fund

The Advisers Act defines a **private fund**. [15 U.S.C. § 80b-2(a)(29)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/80b-2) — the same words as the Form ADV glossary — says a private fund is an **issuer** that would be an investment company as defined in section 3 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 but for section **3(c)(1)** or **3(c)(7)** of that Act.

Those two exclusions are why a single-LP vehicle can still be a fund:

1. [15 U.S.C. § 80a-3(c)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/80a-3) excepts an issuer whose outstanding securities (other than short-term paper) are beneficially owned by **not more than one hundred persons** and that is not making, and does not presently propose to make, a public offering. One beneficial owner sits inside that cap.
2. [15 U.S.C. § 80a-3(c)(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/80a-3) excepts an issuer whose outstanding securities are owned exclusively by **qualified purchasers** at the time of acquisition, and that is not making a public offering. A single qualified purchaser is enough for the count.
3. The SEC’s [Form ADV glossary](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2016/ia-4509-appendix-c.pdf) repeats the statute: **Private Fund** = an issuer that would be an investment company but for 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7).

The test is whether there is an **issuer** of interests. It is not whether a second family showed up.

An SMA is the other design: the sleeve sits in the house’s own name. That overwrite already lives on [separately managed accounts](/separately-managed-account-institutional/). Do not flatten this page onto that keeper. Do not mint a family-office SMA twin.

## What an owner is actually buying

A pension or a family office that signs a fund-of-one is buying a **vehicle** — its own LPA, its own capital account, its own K-1 or equivalent — not a second logo on a commingled book.

A house that opens an SMA is buying a **sleeve**. The securities are titled to the owner. That is why the SMA page and this page are not twins.
