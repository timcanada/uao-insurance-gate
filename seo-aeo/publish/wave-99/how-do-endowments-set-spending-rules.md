# How do endowments set spending rules?

An endowment spending rule is a board appropriation under UPMIFA, not a statutory 5 percent. UPMIFA (2006) lets the institution appropriate so much of an endowment fund as it determines is prudent, using seven factors. An optional 7 percent test is a rebuttable presumption of imprudence — not a safe harbour. IRC § 4942’s 5 percent minimum investment return is a private-foundation test. ## Prudence is not a percentage

The Uniform Law Commission’s [Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act](https://www.nacubo.org/Topics/Endowment-Management/Uniform-Prudent-Management-of-Institutional-Funds-Act) (2006) replaced UMIFA’s historic-dollar-value floor with a prudence rule. Subject to the donor’s intent in the gift instrument, an institution **may appropriate for expenditure or accumulate so much of an endowment fund as the institution determines is prudent** for the uses, benefits, purposes, and duration for which the endowment fund is established.

The same section lists **seven** criteria for that yearly decision: duration and preservation of the endowment fund; the purposes of the institution and the endowment fund; general economic conditions; the effect of inflation or deflation; expected total return from income and appreciation; other resources of the institution; and the investment policy of the institution.

UPMIFA §4(d) is **optional**. A state that adopts it creates a **rebuttable presumption of imprudence** if the institution appropriates more than **seven percent** of the endowment fund’s fair market value in a year, calculated from market values determined **at least quarterly** and averaged over **not less than three years**. The same subsection says it does **not** create a presumption of prudence for an appropriation of **7 percent or less**.

A US **private foundation** is a different legal person. [26 U.S.C. § 4942(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4942) sets the **minimum investment return** at **5 percent** of the excess of non-charitable-use assets over acquisition indebtedness. That is a distribution test for a foundation. It is not UPMIFA, and it is not an endowment board’s spending policy.

Yale’s official [Overview of Yale’s Endowment](https://www.yale.edu/funding-yale-home/overview-yales-endowment) prints a **5.25%** spending rate. Quote the rate. ## What an owner is actually buying

A university board that writes a spending rule is buying a **payout the budget can plan** — a trailing average, a band, or a hybrid — not a second AUM.

A private foundation that meets § 4942 is buying a **statutory minimum**. Calling that “the endowment 5 percent” is how the two jobs get flattened.
