# The Total Portfolio Approach, explained

The total portfolio approach is a way to manage a fund as **one book of risk**, not as a pile of asset-class silos. Every investment competes for capital on what it adds to total-fund risk and return, usually against a simple [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/). CalPERS became the **first US public pension** to adopt it: the board voted on **17 November 2025**; staff launched it on **1 July 2026** against a reference portfolio of **75% global equities and 25% US Treasury bonds**. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

Under a strategic asset allocation (SAA) model, the board sets a fixed weight for each sleeve — so much equity, so much credit, so much private markets — and staff are scored on filling the bucket. Under TPA, the question is: **does this position improve the whole fund relative to a cheap, transparent reference mix?**

CalPERS’ own sentences, 17 November 2025:

- TPA **replaces** the SAA model the system had used to guide investment decisions.
- Under SAA the board periodically allocated a set amount to each asset class.
- Under TPA the focus is which investments can best contribute to the performance of the **entire** portfolio, rather than hitting individual asset-allocation targets.
- Staff get discretion to adjust without those specific targets in place.
- The board adopted a **single** reference portfolio — then described as **75% equities / 25% bonds** — instead of **11** asset-class benchmarks.

The 13 July 2026 preliminary-return release is the live measurement line. CIO **Stephen Gilmore** (who previously oversaw a similar approach at the [New Zealand Super Fund](/new-zealand-superannuation-fund-explained/)): CalPERS will regularly and publicly measure the active book against **75% global equities and 25% US Treasury bonds**.

That is the definition. It is not a new asset class. It is not a promise of extra return.

## Who else this is for

TPA needs scale, an internal bench, and a board that will score the **fund**, not the sleeve heads. That is why it shows up at houses already in the [universal owner](/universal-owner/) set — [NZ Super](/new-zealand-superannuation-fund-explained/), [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/), [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/). Do not invent a “GIC invented TPA in 19xx” origin story that is not on GIC’s own page.

A small family office that still thinks in manager buckets is not “doing TPA” because someone used the words on a slide.

See also [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/) — the benchmark TPA is judged against — and [liability-driven investing](/liability-driven-investing-explained/), which is a different job (hedging a liability, not competing every sleeve against one equity/bond mix).
