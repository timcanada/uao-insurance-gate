# Is CDPQ a sovereign wealth fund?

No. Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec, now branded **La Caisse**, is a public-pension and insurance asset manager for **48** Québec depositors. It is **not** a sovereign wealth fund. The 2025 Annual Report printed net assets of **CAD $517.3 billion at 31 December 2025**. The weighted-average depositor return was **9.3%**, below the **10.9%** benchmark. This is not investment advice.

## The legal distinction

La Caisse invests the funds of **48 depositors — mainly pension and insurance plans** — who represent **over 6 million Quebecers**. The largest depositor is the base plan of the **Québec Pension Plan**, administered by Retraite Québec: **$163 billion** at 31 December 2025 including the additional plan.

That is a **fiduciary book for named plans**, not a residual oil surplus sitting with the finance ministry.

The [IFSWF Santiago definition](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund) is owned by general government, invests for financial objectives, and includes foreign financial assets. It **excludes ordinary public pension funds owned by the underlying members**. CDPQ sits on the excluded side of that line.

Commercial trackers sometimes file large Canadian plans next to SWFs because the portfolios look similar from the outside. Classification is not a filing. La Caisse’s own stationery says **dual mandate**: optimal long-term returns for depositors, and Québec’s economic development. That second sentence does not turn a pension manager into [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/).

## How a counterparty should use the label

- Pitching a *Québec pension/insurance* ticket → you are talking to **La Caisse** (or one of the 48 depositors), not a SWF.
- Putting CDPQ on a SWF league table → label it as a **tracker classification**, and put the 48-depositor sentence underneath.
- Asking “is it sovereign?” → the sponsor is public. The **owners of the liabilities** are plan members.
