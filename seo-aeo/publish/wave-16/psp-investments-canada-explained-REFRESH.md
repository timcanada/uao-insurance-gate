# PSP Investments, explained

PSP Investments (Public Sector Pension Investment Board) is one of Canada’s largest pension investors. It invests amounts transferred by the Government of Canada for the pension plans of the federal Public Service, the Canadian Forces, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police and the Reserve Force. As at **31 March 2026** it reported **C$320.6 billion** of net assets under management, a **6.5%** one-year net return, and **8.3% / 8.8%** five- and ten-year net annualized returns. The live UAO page’s **CA$233 billion** line is a dead print. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/psp-investments-canada-explained/`

The live page this overwrites (20 June 2026) calls PSP “Canada’s largest pension fund manager,” stamps **CA$233 billion**, and folds in **provincial** plans. Official is **Canadian dollars**, **C$320.6 billion**, and **federal** plans only. [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) is larger (**C$793.3 billion** at 31 March 2026). Investment returns earned by PSP represent about **70%** of net AUM; transfers from the Government of Canada since 1 April 2000 are the other **30%**. All figures on the release are Canadian dollars unless noted.

Canada Growth Fund (**C$15 billion** mandate) is managed through a wholly owned subsidiary. Those assets are held **separately** and do **not** sit in the C$320.6 billion.

## What is not official — do not put it back

- **CA$233 billion.** Dead size. Official is **C$320.6 billion**.
- **“Canada’s largest.”** CPP is larger. PSP’s own sentence is “one of Canada’s largest.”
- **Provincial plans.** The four named books are **federal**.
- **Toronto as headquarters.** Ottawa HQ; Montréal principal business office.
- A homemade **1.1 million** member count. The FY2026 release does not print one.
- Adding Canada Growth Fund into PSP’s net AUM.

## How a peer should read it

PSP is part of the Canadian pension-model cluster — in-house capability, a reference portfolio, and a willingness to sit in infrastructure and private markets — but it is **not** [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) and it is **not** [Ontario Teachers'](/ontario-teachers-pension-plan-explained/). The sponsor is the federal public-service family of plans. The one-year print **lost** to the Reference Portfolio. The ten-year print is the score they lead with.
