# Texas Permanent School Fund, explained

The Texas Permanent School Fund is a constitutional endowment for Texas public schools, not a pension and not a sovereign wealth fund. The Texas Permanent School Fund Corporation reported a net position of **$60.6 billion at 31 August 2025**. In that fiscal year it distributed about **$2.2 billion** to the Available School Fund and guaranteed **$138.1 billion** of school-district bonds plus **$5.8 billion** of charter-district bonds. This is not investment advice.

Do not invent a 31 August 2026 NAV. The Corporation’s fiscal year ends 31 August; the next ACFR is the document that will replace $60.6 billion.

Do not invent a single “Texas PSF + GLO land” combined dollar figure. The ACFR is explicit: **Texas PSF** means the Corporation’s book; **the Fund** means that book plus assets managed by the School Land Board and administered by the General Land Office. Until those two are printed together as one official total, keep them apart.

## What it is

The 1845 Texas Constitution created a perpetual school fund. The Corporation’s own site says it was seeded with **$2 million in 1854**. The 87th Legislature created the **Texas Permanent School Fund Corporation** in 2021 (Senate Bill 1232) as a stand-alone special-purpose governmental corporation. That is the legal person that now publishes the ACFR.

Two jobs, both constitutional:

1. **Pay schools.** A total-return distribution to the Available School Fund, not a “spend the coupon” leftover from the land-sale era.
2. **Guarantee school debt.** The Bond Guarantee Program overlays the Fund’s AAA on qualifying school-district and charter-district bonds so those issuers pay less interest. Outstanding guaranteed principal is a *contingent* book — do not add $138.1 billion to $60.6 billion and call it AUM.

This is an **education endowment**, not a teacher pension. [CalSTRS](/calstrs-explained/) pays educators. Texas PSF pays *districts*. Do not file it next to GPIF.

## How a peer should read it

- **$8.3 billion is a howler.** Kill it. The audited Corporation print is **$60.6 billion**.
- **Endowment, not pension.** No funded-status ratio. The constraint is the constitutional distribution test, not a 7% actuarial return.
- **Guarantee is the second product.** A GP who only talks NAV is missing the AAA overlay that actually changes school-district borrowing costs.
- **Corporation ≠ land office.** If a slide mixes GLO mineral income into Corporation NAV without saying so, send it back.
