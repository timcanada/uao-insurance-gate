# New Jersey Division of Investment, explained

The New Jersey Division of Investment is the state investment office for New Jersey’s Pension Fund, not a second CalPERS page and not leftover MassPRIM **$73 billion**. The official [Fiscal Year 2025 Annual Report](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/doinvest/pdf/AnnualReport/AnnualReportforFiscalYear2025.pdf) prints Pension Fund net asset value of **$84,084 million** (**$84.1 billion**) at **30 June 2025**. The Pension Fund returned **10.96 percent** net of all fees. The Cash Management Fund was **$37.76 billion**. Those three lines are not interchangeable. Police and Firemen’s Retirement System assets transferred on **1 April 2024** are no longer in this Pension Fund total. This page is not a ranking and not advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second CalPERS overwrite or a second PSERS overwrite.** Those keepers are `/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/` and `/pennsylvania-psers/`. Do not mint `/njdoi/`, `/new-jersey-pension-fund/`, or `/new-jersey-division-of-investment-explained/`. Do not add the Cash Management Fund to the Pension Fund and call the sum New Jersey’s pension.

## What is official (30 June 2025)

The Division’s own report is the sourced split:

1. **Pension Fund net asset value** **$84,084 million** at **30 June 2025** (2024: **$78,771 million**). Named books on that table include Teachers’ Pension and Annuity Fund **$34,054 million** and Public Employees’ Retirement System **$39,643 million**. Do not treat leftover MassPRIM **$73 billion** or leftover Ohio PERS **$46 billion** as this filing.
2. **What left the book.** P.L. 2018, c. 55 moved investment-policy authority for the Police and Firemen’s Retirement System. The Division transferred PFRS’s proportional share of Common Pension Fund D as of **1 April 2024**. The Fiscal Year 2024 report printed that transfer at **$20.8 billion**. Later Pension Fund totals exclude those transferred assets. Do not add them back.
3. **Return and cash flows.** Pension Fund return **10.96 percent** net of all fees against a **11.70 percent** benchmark. About **$10.3 billion** paid to plan beneficiaries. Contributions: State **$6.5 billion** (including net lottery proceeds), local employers **$4.3 billion**, employees **$2.5 billion**. Cash Management Fund net asset value **$37.76 billion**. The January 2026 State Investment Council presentation prints a Division total of **$123,318 million** across the Pension Fund, Cash Management Fund, deferred compensation, NJBEST, SACT, and the Trustees for the Support of Public Schools Fund. That total is not the Pension Fund.

Do not flatten the Division into [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) or [PSERS](/pennsylvania-psers/). CalPERS is California. PSERS is one Pennsylvania school-employee file. This is **one New Jersey investment office**.

## What an owner is actually buying

Counsel selling “New Jersey pension AUM” has to name the book. The **$84.1 billion** line is the Pension Fund the Division still manages. The **$37.76 billion** line is cash management. The **$123.3 billion** line is a Division stack. Those are not the same mandate.
