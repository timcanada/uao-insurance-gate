# What is a chokepoint for a universal owner?

A chokepoint is a geography-and-volume fact, not a Probability Desk weight. The U.S. Energy Information Administration defines world oil transit chokepoints as narrow channels along widely used global sea routes that are critical to energy trade. In the first half of 2025, world liquids supply was 104.4 million barrels a day; about 76 percent (79.8 million barrels a day) moved seaborne. The Strait of Malacca was the largest oil-transit chokepoint at 23.2 million barrels a day — 29 percent of maritime oil. The Strait of Hormuz moved 20.9 million barrels a day, about 20 percent of global petroleum-liquids consumption and one-quarter of maritime traded oil, plus 11.4 billion cubic feet a day of LNG — more than 20 percent of global LNG trade. This page is not a ranking, not a forecast, and not advice.

## Geography first, then a weight

EIA’s [World Oil Transit Chokepoints](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/special_topics/World_Oil_Transit_Chokepoints) note (last updated **3 March 2026**) is the sourced definition:

1. Chokepoints are **narrow channels** along widely used global sea routes. They matter because of the petroleum, other liquids, and LNG that pass through them. A blockage, even temporary, can delay supply and raise shipping costs.
2. In **1H25**, world petroleum and other liquids supply was about **104.4 million b/d**. EIA estimates about **76 percent** of that (**79.8 million b/d**) moved as seaborne trade. The **Strait of Malacca** carried **23.2 million b/d** — **29 percent** of maritime oil, and the largest chokepoint by oil-transit volume. The **Strait of Hormuz** carried **20.9 million b/d**, about **20 percent** of global petroleum-liquids consumption and **one-quarter** of maritime traded oil. **11.4 Bcf/d** of LNG moved through Hormuz — **more than 20 percent** of global LNG trade.
3. Saudi Aramco’s East-West crude pipeline and the UAE’s Abu Dhabi pipeline together could provide about **4.7 million b/d** of Hormuz-bypass capacity. The **Cape of Good Hope is not a chokepoint**. EIA prints it as a major trade route and the **alternative** to other chokepoints.
