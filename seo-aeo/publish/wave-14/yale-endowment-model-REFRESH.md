# The Yale Endowment Model, explained

The Yale Endowment Model is the diversified, equity-heavy, alternatives-heavy framework **David Swensen** built at Yale: a perpetual spending institution that takes illiquidity because it can, and pays a smoothed **5.25%** target toward the operating budget. Yale News printed the fiscal-2025 score on **24 October 2025**: **11.1%** net, **$4.5 billion** of investment gains, **$2.1 billion** distributed, endowment value **$44.1 billion at 30 June 2025**. The CIO named on that release is **Matt Mendelsohn**. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/yale-endowment-model/`

The live page this overwrites still says the endowment was **approximately $41.4 billion as of June 2023**, reprints a **2023** target-allocation table (33% private equity, 3% fixed income), and then ranks Harvard at **$50.9 billion** and CalPERS at **$469 billion** from the same vintage. Yale’s own FY2025 print has moved. [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) PERF is **$637.1 billion** at 30 June 2026 — and CalPERS is a public pension, not an endowment. ## What the model actually is

Swensen’s job, from **1985** until his death in **2021** ([Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/22/david-swensens-coda)), was to fund a **perpetual** university: spend enough this year, keep real purchasing power, take risks a 60/40 daily-liquidity book will not. The operating features that are still on Yale’s own pages:

- **Equity bias.** Inflation and a 5.25% spending target push the pool away from a large bond buffer.
- **Illiquidity as a feature.** A university that spends a smoothed fraction of market value can hold private partnerships a public pension with a monthly payroll cannot.
- **External managers, internal governance.** The financial report: marketable alternatives, private equity (venture and buyouts), real assets (real estate, timber, energy, minerals).
- **Smoothing.** This year’s spend is **80%** of last year’s spend plus **20%** of the 5.25% target applied to starting market value. That is why spending can rise when the NAV has been flat.

It is **not** a [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/). Yale *compares* itself, in the October release, with “a typical 70/30 stock and bond portfolio” over ten years (**+2.2%** a year). That is a talking-point hurdle, not a CalPERS-style 75/25 governance benchmark.

It is **not** a [total portfolio approach](/total-portfolio-approach/) mandate. TPA is a public-pension governance model scored on one cheap mix. Yale is an endowment spending model scored on mission support.

It is **not** a licence for a $500 million family office to copy 91% equity-like alternatives and call the result “Yale.” Scale, access, and a 5.25% smoothed spend are the constraints.

## What it is not

- **Not CalPERS.** PERF **$637.1 billion** at 30 June 2026. Different legal person, different board, different [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/).
- **Not a sovereign wealth fund.** See [what is a SWF](/sovereign-wealth-fund/).

- **Not GPFG.** Norway’s official book is **22,683 billion kroner** at 30 June 2026 — a savings SWF, not an endowment model exhibit.

## How a peer should read it

- **$41.4 billion is FY2024, not “as of June 2023.”** OIR’s FY2023 print was **$40,504 million**. The live page skipped a year and then froze.
- **11.1% lagged public indices.** Mendelsohn said so. Quote both facts.
- **5.25% is the Corporation target, not the cash yield on NAV.** Smoothing is the product.
