# SWF vs pension: GPFG is not “more than $2 trillion” — and GPIF is the pension

A sovereign wealth fund invests national wealth for a written public mandate. A pension fund invests to pay **named members**. Those are different legal jobs even when a government sits behind both. The live page still says Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global “alone holds **more than $2 trillion**.” Official is **22,683 billion kroner** at **30 June 2026**. Do not dollarize it so a comparison can have a winner.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

Print kroner for GPFG and yen for GPIF. The TAI / WTW top-300 figure is a **study vintage**, already used on [`/what-is-a-pension-fund/`](/what-is-a-pension-fund/) and [`/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/`](/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/). It is not an official GPFG AUM.

## What the comparison can say

[A SWF](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) is owned by a government and invested against a public mandate — savings, stabilisation, reserves, or development. [A pension fund](/what-is-a-pension-fund/) is held for members. Accountability runs to trustees and beneficiaries, not to a finance ministry’s savings rule.

GPFG is the large public exhibit of the first job. [GPIF](/gpif-japan-explained/) is the large public exhibit of the second. Norway’s book is **not** a member pension. Japan’s book is **not** a SWF. A page that needs GPFG **more than $2 trillion** to decide “which is bigger” is answering the wrong question.

## What is not official

- GPFG **more than $2 trillion**, or any other homemade dollar.
- GPIF as a sovereign wealth fund.
- CPP as a sovereign wealth fund.
- A homemade “category vs single fund” winner that dollarizes kroner.
