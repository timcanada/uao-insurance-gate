# Cbus, explained

Cbus is the **Construction and Building Unions Superannuation Fund**, an Australian industry super fund that commenced in **1984**. Its official fund-information page prints **more than $115 billion** of funds under management, **more than 935,000** members and **more than 230,000** employers at **30 June 2026**. The live UAO page’s **$60 billion / $63.3 billion** lines and **2.5 million** member count are dead. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/cbus-superannuation-explained/`

Cbus merged with Media Super in **April 2022** and with EISS Super in **May 2023** (17,000 members on that release). Those are official merger facts on Cbus media pages. They are not a second FUM.

## What is not official — do not put it back

- **$60 billion** or **AUD 63.3 billion as of 30 June 2024.** Dead.
- **2.5 million** members. Official is **935,000**.
- A **CFMEU governance model** as the legal structure. Official name is the Construction and Building Unions Superannuation Fund, with a licensed trustee.
- **1983** founding. Official commencement is **1984**.
- “Australia’s largest industry super fund.” [AustralianSuper](/australiansuper-explained/) is larger.
- Minting `/cbus-super/` or a third explained slug.

## How a peer should read it

Cbus is the building-trades industry fund — 1984, a licensed trustee, more than **230,000** participating employers. The score they print today is **more than $115 billion** at 30 June 2026. Anyone still writing **$60 billion** is a year-plus and about **$50 billion** late.
