# Nevada Public Employees' Retirement System, explained

The Public Employees’ Retirement System of Nevada is Nevada’s public-employee pension, not leftover MassPRIM **$73 billion** and not a second CalPERS page. The official [2025 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report](https://www.nvpers.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/FY25ACFRFinal.pdf) prints fiduciary net position restricted for pension benefits of **$70,797,644,282** at **30 June 2025**. The investment program returned **11.0 percent** time-weighted, gross of fees. The money-weighted return was **10.93 percent** net of fees. Those two return lines are not interchangeable. This page is not a ranking and not advice.

## What is official (30 June 2025)

The statement is the sourced print:

1. **Fiduciary net position restricted for pension benefits** **$70,797,644,282** at **30 June 2025** (2024: **$64,455,555,418**). Do not treat leftover MassPRIM **$73 billion**, leftover Colorado PERA **$67 billion**, or leftover Ohio PERS **$46 billion** as this filing.
2. **Named books on the same report.** The System is two sub-funds — Regular and Police/Fire. Assets of the System can legally be used to pay both Regular and Police/Fire beneficiaries. This desk does not invent a Regular-versus-Police/Fire AUM split that the combining statement does not print. Do not add a homemade Regular book to a homemade Police/Fire book and call the sum a second AUM.
3. **Returns and GASB.** Time-weighted return **11.0 percent** gross of fees. Money-weighted return **10.93 percent** net of fees. Those two lines are not interchangeable. Plan fiduciary net position as a percentage of total pension liability **80.5 percent**. Net pension liability **$17,185,295,625**. Those GASB lines are not a second AUM.

Do not flatten Nevada PERS into [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) or [Colorado PERA](/colorado-pera/). CalPERS is California. Colorado PERA is Colorado. This is **one Nevada public-employee file**.
