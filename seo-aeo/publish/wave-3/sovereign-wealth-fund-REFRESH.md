# What Is a Sovereign Wealth Fund?

A sovereign wealth fund is a government-owned investment vehicle that invests national wealth for long-term public objectives, separately from the annual budget and from day-to-day central-bank reserves. It is usually funded by commodity revenue or trade surpluses and can hold equities, bonds, private markets and real assets. Universal Asset Owners uses the IFSWF / Santiago definition: state-owned, financial objectives, including foreign financial assets. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Owned by** | A government (national or, less often, sub-national) |
| **Job** | Invest a ring-fenced pool for a written mandate — savings, stabilisation, reserves, development, or pension pre-funding |
| **Not the same as** | Central-bank FX reserves, or a public pension that owes named beneficiaries |
| **How big** | Industry trackers put the universe around **$13–15 trillion** in 2025; treat any total as approximate. See [largest sovereign wealth funds](/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds/). |

## What the official definition actually says

The [International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund) publishes the definition the funds wrote for themselves in the 2008 Santiago Principles. Three tests do the work:

1. Owned by the general government.
2. Invests for financial objectives.
3. Includes investments in foreign financial assets.

That definition **excludes** ordinary central-bank reserves held only for monetary policy, ordinary state-owned enterprises, and government-employee pension funds owned by the underlying members. If a desk fails those tests, do not call it a SWF to win a search query.

## How they are funded

- **Commodity.** Oil, gas, minerals. The Gulf, Norway, and other resource states. The job is often to turn a depleting resource into a permanent financial endowment.
- **Non-commodity / surplus.** Persistent current-account surpluses and excess reserves. The Singapore and China pattern: earn more than a reserve portfolio would.

The funding source is not trivia. It is why one fund can sit in private markets for a decade and another must stay liquid for the budget.

## Five types (many funds are hybrids)

1. **Stabilisation** — smooth the budget; liquidity first.
2. **Savings / future generations** — keep today’s resource income alive for people not yet born.
3. **Reserve investment** — a higher-return sleeve on reserves the central bank does not need for day-to-day stability.
4. **Development / strategic** — domestic industry, infrastructure, national champions. See [strategic investment funds](/strategic-investment-funds/).
5. **Pension reserve** — pre-fund public pensions; sits on the border with [pension funds](/what-is-a-pension-fund/).

## SWF vs reserves vs pension

| | Sovereign wealth fund | Central-bank reserves | Public pension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Long-term return on national wealth | Monetary stability | Pay defined benefits |
| Horizon | Years to generations | Short, liquid | Decades, liability-driven |
| Typical book | Equities, bonds, private and real assets | High-grade bonds, gold, FX | Diversified; growing alternatives |
| Who is owed | Often no named beneficiary | The monetary system | Members and retirees |

Fuller comparisons: [SWF vs pension](/sovereign-wealth-fund-vs-pension-fund/), [reserves vs SWFs](/reserve-funds-vs-sovereign-wealth-funds/).

## Governance

Because these books are state-owned and invest across borders, counterparties care about process. The voluntary code is the [Santiago Principles](/santiago-principles/) — 24 principles on legal framework, governance, and investment and risk management, now stewarded by IFSWF.

## Why universal owners care

The largest SWFs are diversified enough that they cannot sell “the system.” That is the [universal owner](/universal-owner/) problem: returns depend on the economy they already hold. For a CIO, the useful question is not “is this exotic?” It is “which mandate, which withdrawal rule, which political owner?”

## FAQ

**What is a sovereign wealth fund in one sentence?**  
A government-owned, professionally managed investment pool, kept apart from the budget and from day-to-day reserves, invested against a written long-term mandate.

**How many are there?**  
IFSWF and commercial trackers count on the order of 90–100+ funds depending on the definition. The exact census changes with new vehicles and with what you include.

**Who is the largest?**  
Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global, managed by [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/), is the transparent benchmark and is generally treated as the largest single SWF. Rankings below Norway are estimates. Use [our league table](/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds/) and read the as-of dates.
