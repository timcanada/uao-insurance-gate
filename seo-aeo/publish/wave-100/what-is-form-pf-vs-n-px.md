# What is Form PF vs Form N-PX?

Form PF is a confidential private-fund adviser filing. Form N-PX is a public proxy-vote report. A registered adviser with at least $150 million in private-fund assets must file Form PF. Large hedge-fund advisers at $1.5 billion file quarterly. Enhanced N-PX, adopted 2 November 2022, makes registered funds report proxy votes and makes 13F managers report say-on-pay. Neither form is a public pension’s holdings PDF. This page is not a ranking and not filing advice.

## Two statutes, two rooms

[17 CFR § 275.204(b)-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.204(b)-1) is the Form PF trigger. If you are an investment adviser registered or required to be registered under Advisers Act section 203, you advise one or more private funds, and as of the end of your most recently completed fiscal year you managed private-fund assets of at least **$150 million**, you must file Form PF (17 CFR 279.9) on IARD.

The SEC’s [Form PF instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/formpf.pdf) add the scale tests an owner actually meets in a side letter:

1. The **$150 million** private-fund AUM test is measured with related persons as of the last day of the adviser’s most recently completed fiscal year.
2. A **large hedge fund adviser** — at least **$1.5 billion** in hedge-fund assets with related persons — files **Section 2** and updates **every quarter**. A large private-equity adviser at **$2 billion** in private-equity-fund assets has its own extra section.
3. The Commission **does not intend to make public** information reported on Form PF that is identifiable to any particular adviser or private fund, though it may use the file in an enforcement action. Advisers Act section 204(b) is a systemic-risk file for FSOC, not a press PDF.

Form N-PX is the other room. The SEC [adopted enhanced N-PX on 2 November 2022](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-198) (Release 33-11131). Registered management investment companies already reported proxy votes on N-PX. The amendments make that record more usable. New Exchange Act **rule 14Ad-1** requires each institutional investment manager that must file under Exchange Act **section 13(f)** to report annually on Form N-PX each **say-on-pay** vote over which it exercised voting power — executive compensation, the frequency of those votes, and golden-parachute votes. The first filings under the amendments were due in **2024**.

A public pension that posts its own book is not filing either form. That job already sits on [how public pensions publish holdings](/how-do-public-pensions-publish-holdings/). Do not paste CalPERS’ partnership report onto Form PF.

## What an owner is actually buying

An allocator reading a manager is buying **two different files**. Form PF is the confidential systemic file — the owner does not get a copy from EDGAR. N-PX is the public vote file for registered funds and, for say-on-pay, for 13F managers.

A governmental plan sitting outside ERISA Title I does not become a Form PF filer by publishing holdings. The administrator job already sits on [what an administrator is for a pension plan](/what-is-an-administrator-for-a-pension-plan/).
