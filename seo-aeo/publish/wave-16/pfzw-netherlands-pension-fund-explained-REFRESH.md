# PFZW (Netherlands), explained

PFZW (**Pensioenfonds Zorg en Welzijn**) is the Dutch sector pension fund for healthcare and welfare. The official English annual-report summary for **2025** prints assets under management of **€252,036 million** at **31 December 2025**, an actual funding ratio of **126.0%**, and **3,037,700** participants and pensioners. The live UAO page’s **€35 billion / 1.2 million / 2023** lines are dead. PFZW transitioned to the new Dutch pension scheme on **1 January 2026**. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/pfzw-netherlands-pension-fund-explained/`

The live page this overwrites (21 June 2026) invents the legal name “Pensioenfonds Ziekenhuizen en Welzijnsinstellingen,” stamps **approximately €35 billion as of 2023**, cuts the membership to **1.2 million**, and names the AFM as the supervisor. Official name is **Zorg en Welzijn**. Official AUM is **€252.0 billion**. The English summary’s discount-rate footnote cites the term structure published by **De Nederlandsche Bank**. Twins `/pfzw-and-pggm-netherlands-explained/` and `/pfzw-pggm-netherlands/` are still 200. 301 those here after paste. PGGM is the **administrator**, not a second AUM.

## What is not official — do not put it back

- **€35 billion / €35.2 billion as of 2023.** Off by roughly **€217 billion**.
- **1.2 million** members. Official total is **3.04 million** including deferred and pensioners.
- **“Pensioenfonds Ziekenhuizen en Welzijnsinstellingen.”** Wrong expansion. The fund’s own name is **Zorg en Welzijn**.
- **AFM as the prudential supervisor.** The English summary’s liability discount cites **DNB**.
- Homemade [ABP](/abp-netherlands-pension-fund-explained/) **€565 billion**. Do not invent a peer size here.
- A 40/35 sleeve split. Not in the 2025 English summary.

## How a peer should read it

PFZW is a **sector pension**, not a sovereign wealth fund and not PGGM. PGGM executes. The 2025 return is **negative** (−3.8%) while the funding ratio **rose** (rate effect on liabilities). On 1 January 2026 the book moved onto the new Dutch contract: pensions can rise and fall faster; a solidarity reserve is there to blunt cuts. That is a **regime change**, not a new AUM.
