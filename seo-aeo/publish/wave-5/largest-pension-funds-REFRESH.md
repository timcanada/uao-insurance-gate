# The World's Largest Pension Funds

The world’s largest dedicated public pension fund is Japan’s Government Pension Investment Fund: **¥293,427.6 billion at the end of December 2025**, and **¥293,643.7 billion of investment assets at the end of fiscal 2025 (31 March 2026)**. Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global is larger on some dollar league tables, but it is a **sovereign wealth fund** funded by petroleum revenue, not a scheme that owes named members. Thinking Ahead Institute / WTW put the top 300 pension funds at a record about **US$24.4 trillion**. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/`

## The classification problem (read this first)

Every “largest pension fund” argument is really an argument about **perimeter**.

| Book | Official size | Is it a pension? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPIF (Japan) | ¥293,427.6 billion (31 Dec 2025); ¥293,643.7 billion investment assets (31 Mar 2026) | Yes. National pension reserve for Japan’s public pension system. | 
| Government Pension Fund Global (NBIM) | **22,683 billion kroner** (30 Jun 2026) | **No, not in the Santiago / IFSWF sense of a member pension.** Petroleum saver with “pension” in the name. Rank it on [largest SWFs](/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds/). |
| National Pension Service (Korea), ABP (Netherlands), CalPERS, CPP / CPPI, Australian super funds | Own filings, different dates | Yes — public or industry pensions / super with beneficiaries |

If a graphic puts Norway first *as a pension*, it has chosen the name over the liability. If it puts Japan first, it has chosen the beneficiary test. UAO uses the beneficiary test on this page and the SWF tests on the other.

## Official numbers at the top

**GPIF — official.** The Q3 FY2025 English pack (published 6 February 2026) reports **total assets ¥293,427.6 billion** at 31 December 2025. The FY2025 annual-report summary reports **cumulative returns from FY2001 of ¥196,930.6 billion** and **investment assets of ¥293,643.7 billion** at 31 March 2026. The policy mix remains the four-way 25% bands: domestic bonds, foreign bonds, domestic equities, foreign equities. Alternatives were **1.70%** of the book at end-December 2025, inside a 5% cap.

Do not convert those yen figures to a fake-precise US dollar headline on this page. The yen/dollar rate can move GPIF’s *dollar* rank by hundreds of billions without a change in the yen book. That is why Norway can “overtake” Japan on a dollar study and lose again on the next print.

**GPFG — official, but the wrong list.** NBIM’s fund value was **22,683 billion kroner at 30 June 2026** (9.4% in the first half). End-2025 was 21,268 billion kroner. Use those numbers on the [SWF table](/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds/).

**The industry study.** Thinking Ahead Institute / WTW’s Global Top 300 has been the citation for “top 300 at a record **US$24.4 trillion**” and for the 2025 order-swap in which Norway’s petroleum fund printed ahead of GPIF in *dollar* terms. The same institute’s Asset Owner 100 (assets at **end-2024**) had NBIM at US$1.7 trillion and GPIF at US$1.6 trillion as the two largest *asset owners* of any kind. Those are study vintages, not live NAV.

## The rest of the top tier (named, not invented)

Below the Japan / Norway argument, the same names recur on every serious list. Print them as institutions, not as a fake-precise dollar ladder, unless you are looking at that fund’s own filing on that date:

- **Korea — National Pension Service.** A member pension. Do not confuse it with [KIC](/korea-investment-corporation-kic/), which is the SWF (USD 232.0 billion at end-December 2025).
- **Netherlands — ABP** (and PFZW). Large, liability-aware, European public-sector pensions.
- **United States — Federal Thrift Savings Plan**, then the big state systems (CalPERS, CalSTRS, NY Common, Florida SBA, Texas Teachers). Use each plan’s own CAFR / valuation date; do not freeze a magazine dollar figure here.
- **Canada — CPP Investments** (the investment board) and the large provincial plans (Ontario Teachers, OMERS, [PSP Investments](/psp-investments/), AIMCo, BCI). “The Canadian model” is in-house alternatives at pension scale.
- **Singapore — Central Provident Fund.** A national defined-contribution system, not a SWF. GIC and Temasek are the reserve / commercial pair.
- **China — National Social Security Fund.** A pension reserve, not CIC.
- **Australia — [AustralianSuper](/australian-super/)** and the other large AUM supers (AustralianSuper itself: more than A$410 billion, 3.6 million members, 31 December 2025). Australia’s [Future Fund](https://www.futurefund.gov.au/) is the SWF (A$269.1 billion at 31 March 2026), not a member super.

## Concentration

The TAI / WTW top-300 work is also the source for the concentration story the live page already told: the **top 20** holding a rising share of the top 300 (the last widely cited cut put the top 20 around **US$10.3 trillion**, about 42% of the 300). That is the allocator fact. A handful of boards now set the fee, stewardship, and private-markets maths for a material slice of retirement capital.

Those boards are [universal owners](/universal-owner/) whether or not they use the phrase. They cannot sell the system.

## How the giants invest (without turning this into a strategy note)

- **GPIF** is built to be simple at scale: four liquid sleeves, tight policy bands, low turnover, alternatives capped.
- **Canadian and some European plans** run more internal private markets — infrastructure, real estate, private equity — because they have the staff and the liability horizon.
- **US publics** are a spread: TSP is a defined-contribution menu; CalPERS is an active steward at public-plan scale. Read the plan, not the country.

Fees, governance, and the discount-rate convention matter more at this size than a clever year.

## Funded status is not one number

US *corporate* defined-benefit plans and US *public* plans do not use the same liability discount. A surplus on a Milliman corporate-100 print and a deficit on a public GASB print can describe the same market week. See UAO’s funded-status and discount-rate explainers before quoting a 2026 “pensions are fine / pensions are broken” line.

## For investment committees and service providers

- Decide the perimeter before you argue about rank.
- Prefer official yen / kroner / local currency with a date over a dollar graphic.
- Do not pitch a SWF product to a pension IC, or a pension product to a SWF, because a league table put them in the same cell.
- Concentration means a small number of relationships now dominate the institutional market. That is a commercial fact, not a reason to flatten the mandates.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the largest pension fund in the world?**  
On a beneficiary definition: **GPIF**. On a “largest book with pension in the name” definition: often **GPFG**. UAO uses the first.

**Did Norway overtake Japan?**  
On some **US-dollar** studies in 2025, yes. In yen, GPIF’s own book was still ¥293.4 trillion at end-December 2025 and ¥293.6 trillion of investment assets at 31 March 2026. FX is doing a lot of the work.

**How much do the largest pension funds manage together?**  
TAI / WTW: top 300 about **US$24.4 trillion** in the last record cut those houses published for 2025. Confirm the vintage before you put it in a slide.

**Is CalPERS the largest US pension?**  
Largest *state* defined-benefit system, yes, on the usual reading. The Federal Thrift Savings Plan is larger than CalPERS on several industry tables because it is the federal DC scheme. Use the latest official filing for either, not a remembered headline.
