# Placement agents for sovereign funds, pensions and family offices

Asset owners meet placement agents when a GP is raising, a secondaries book is being shown, or a co-invest sleeve needs a door that is not a cold PDF. This hub is Universal Asset Owners' map of that category — written for the desk that takes the meeting, and usable by the firms that want to be found. It is not a ranking and not fundraising advice.

The related job of sitting alongside a GP on a deal is already defined on [What is co-investment in private equity?](/what-is-co-investment-in-private-equity/). **Do not treat this directory page as a second definition of co-investment, secondaries, or investment banking.** A listing here is not an endorsement. Featured placements are marked paid.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## What owners are actually buying

A public pension that takes a placement-agent call is buying a process the board can minute: who was shown the book, who was conflicted, and why this vintage is in the policy. That is a [fiduciary](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/) file, not a lunch.

A sovereign or strategic fund that takes the call is buying access that matches the mandate — savings, development, or a named sleeve — not a retail roadshow.

A family office that takes the call is buying confidentiality first. The principal will not tolerate a leak, and a megafund raise is sometimes the wrong meeting for that desk.

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) hold capital for their own account. The agent works a raise. Those are different jobs. Do not invent a house AUM for an agent, a GP, or an owner on this page.

If you sell into this room, say which of those meetings you have closed. If you buy, ask for the last three mandates *like yours*, redacted, and who did the work.

## The questions a CIO or allocator should ask

1. Who is the working partner, and have they sat on your side of the investment committee?
2. Who pays you, and where does that sit in the GP's fee stack?
3. How do you handle a conflict when you also cover a competitor or a secondaries buyer?
4. What will you refuse to put in the data-room memo?
