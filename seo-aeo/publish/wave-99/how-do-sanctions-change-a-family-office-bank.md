# How do sanctions change a family-office bank?

A family office is not an OFAC exemption. A US bank must block property of blocked persons and treat an entity owned 50 percent or more in the aggregate by blocked persons as itself blocked — even when that entity is not on the SDN list. When the office opens a legal-entity account, FinCEN’s CDD rule requires the bank to identify each 25 percent owner and one control person. This page is not a ranking and not sanctions advice.

## The bank is the US person

OFAC’s revised [50 Percent Rule guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/6186/download?inline=) (**13 August 2014**) and [FAQ 399](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/399) (same date) state the ownership test a family-office bank actually runs:

1. Blocked persons are considered to have an interest in all property of an entity they own, individually or **in the aggregate**, directly or indirectly, at **50 percent or greater**.
2. Any such entity is **itself considered a blocked person**. Its property is blocked **whether or not** the entity is listed on OFAC’s Specially Designated Nationals list.
3. FAQ 399’s example: if Blocked Person X owns 25 percent of Entity A and Blocked Person Y owns another 25 percent, Entity A is blocked because aggregate ownership is 50 percent. Ownership interests under **different** OFAC programmes are aggregated.

A US bank that holds the family book is a US person. A private constitution, a family-office exemption under the Advisers Act, or a “we are not a bank” sentence does not remove that duty.

The account-opening file is a second statute. [31 CFR § 1010.230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.230) (81 FR 29451, **11 May 2016**) requires covered financial institutions — including banks — to identify and verify beneficial owners of **legal entity customers** when a new account is opened. Beneficial owner means:

- each individual who owns, directly or indirectly, **25 percent or more** of the equity interests; and
- **one** individual with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the entity.

A family LLC, partnership, or foreign analogue that files with a secretary of state (or a similar foreign office) is a legal entity customer unless an exclusion in paragraph (e) applies. A family office is not on that exclusion list as a class.

## What an owner is actually buying

A family office that keeps a US bank is buying a **screen** — SDN plus the 50 percent look-through — and a **beneficial-owner file**. Losing the account is often a beneficial-owner or a 50-percent problem, not a “the banker does not like family offices” story.
