## What the definition can say

A **reserve fund** in the monetary-policy sense is a central-bank or finance-ministry book held for FX intervention and short-horizon stability. It must stay liquid. A [sovereign wealth fund](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) is a longer-horizon investment mandate, ring-fenced from day-to-day intervention. [Stabilisation](/stabilisation-vs-heritage-fund-mandates/) funds sit in between: they exist to smooth a budget when commodity revenue falls. Chile’s [ESSF](/chile-economic-and-social-stabilization-fund-explained/) is that job. Timor-Leste’s [Petroleum Fund](/timor-leste-petroleum-fund/) is a petroleum savings rule. MAS official foreign reserves are the Singapore exhibit — already split from GIC and Temasek on [`/singapore-investment-landscape-gic-temasek-mas/`](/singapore-investment-landscape-gic-temasek-mas/).

The live twin [`/reserve-fund-sovereign/`](/reserve-fund-sovereign/) makes the same error with older dollars: GPFG **$1.3 trillion**, ESSF **$17 billion**, Timor **$19 billion**, an Abu Dhabi leftover **SGRF $4 billion**, Russia NWF **$183 billion**, SOFAZ **$48 billion**, and Alberta Heritage **$42 billion**. Oman’s SGRF was assembled into OIA in **2020**. It is not an Abu Dhabi reserve sleeve. **301 that twin after this paste.** Do not mint `/reserve-funds-vs-sovereign-wealth-funds/` (404).
