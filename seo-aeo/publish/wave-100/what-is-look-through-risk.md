# What is look-through risk?

Look-through risk is the risk you cannot see — or must capitalise — once a fund is opened. Under Solvency II Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35 Article 84, an insurer calculates the Solvency Capital Requirement on each underlying asset of a collective investment undertaking or packaged fund. If that look-through cannot be applied, the rule allows a target or last-reported allocation, and limits data groupings to 20 percent of total assets. That is not OFAC’s 50 percent ownership test and not a custodian tape. This page is not a ranking and not capital advice.

## Open the fund or pay the charge

[Article 84](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/rulebook/solvency-ii-single-rulebook/article-2490_en) of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) **2015/35** (10 October 2014, supplementing the Solvency II Directive) is the sourced definition:

1. The Solvency Capital Requirement **shall be calculated on the basis of each of the underlying assets** of collective investment undertakings and other investments packaged as funds — the **look-through approach**.
2. The same look-through also applies to **indirect** exposures to market risk other than CIUs, to underwriting risk, and to counterparty risk.
3. Where look-through cannot be applied, the SCR **may** be calculated on the target underlying asset allocation or, if that is not available, the last reported asset allocation — provided the assets are managed in accordance with that allocation and exposures are not expected to vary materially over a short period. **Data groupings** may be used if they let every relevant standard-formula sub-module run and do not apply to more than **20 percent** of the undertaking’s total assets.

EIOPA’s [Guidelines on the look-through approach](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/document/download/7082c224-1092-4ed4-ba53-7ba7ff04030f_en?filename=Guidelines+on+look-through+approach.pdf) apply from **1 April 2015**. They tell supervisors to apply the same method to real estate held through a fund.

That is a **capital** look-through. OFAC’s 50 percent test is an **ownership** look-through. A custodian “look-through” is a **tape** that matches the board pack. Do not flatten those three jobs. The insurer-versus-pension keeper already has Solvency II in force **January 2016** and SCR at **99.5 percent** VaR over one year. This page is the fund-opening rule inside that capital charge.

## What an owner is actually buying

An insurer buying a CIU is buying a file of **names the standard formula can charge**. If the manager will not open the book, the owner pays a cruder charge — or does not buy.

A pension or a family office that asks for look-through is usually buying the **tape**, not Article 84.
