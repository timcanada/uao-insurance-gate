# New York City Retirement Systems, explained

The New York City Retirement Systems are the City’s five public pension funds. Comptroller **Mark Levine** and the systems’ trustees reported a combined **$326.3 billion** at **30 June 2026** and a **13%** aggregate return net of fees for the fiscal year ending that date, against a **7.0%** actuarial target. The Comptroller’s office calls the five systems collectively “the third largest public pension system in the nation.” This is **not** [New York State’s Common Retirement Fund](/new-york-state-common-retirement-fund-explained/). This is not investment advice.

## What it is

Five legal persons. One combined print. The Bureau of Asset Management invests on behalf of the systems; the Comptroller is custodian, delegated investment advisor, and a trustee. Each system still has its own trustees.

This is a US public pension cluster, not a sovereign wealth fund. Compare it with [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) and [CalSTRS](/calstrs-explained/), and keep it distinct from [NYSCRF](/new-york-state-common-retirement-fund-explained/). New York City’s fiscal year ends **30 June**. New York State’s fund year ends **31 March**. Do not mash the two books.

## How a peer should read it

- **$326.3 billion is the combined FY2026 print.** Anyone writing five invented system totals is not reading the filing.
- **13% is net of fees, against 7.0%.** The $6.3 billion line is an estimated City contribution effect beginning FY28, not a NAV.
- **Not the State fund.** DiNapoli’s Common Retirement Fund is a different house, a different fiscal year, and a different trustee model.
- **“Third largest” is the Comptroller’s claim.** Attribute it. Do not invent the rest of the league table.
