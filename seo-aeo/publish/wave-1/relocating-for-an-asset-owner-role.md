# Relocating for an asset-owner role — what actually changes

Moving city for a sovereign fund, public pension, endowment or family office is not a banking posting with a different logo. The horizon, the accountability and the reason the institution sits where it sits all change. This Careers Intelligence briefing is the umbrella for Universal Asset Owners' city pages — Abu Dhabi, Singapore, Oslo, Toronto, London, Riyadh and the desks that follow. It is not immigration or tax advice.

## The move is part of the mandate

Norges Bank Investment Management sits in Oslo because the fund is a Norwegian public institution. GIC and Temasek sit in Singapore because that is the state's investment architecture. CPP Investments and Ontario Teachers' sit in Toronto because that is where the sponsors built them — and then they opened London, New York and Hong Kong because the book required it. PIF sits in Riyadh because Vision-era national capital sits there.

If you treat the city as a lifestyle upgrade, you will misread the job. Owners already know that retention fails when people “come for a few years but don't always stay” — a point our [sovereign-fund playbook](/careers/intelligence/moving-into-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/) already makes.

## What to pressure-test before you accept

1. **Decision rights.** Are you taking risk, or preparing decks for a committee you will not sit on?
2. **Publicness.** A US public pension and a Gulf fund have different newspaper problems and the same need for process.
3. **Family.** Schooling, spouses who cannot work, and parents who will not board the plane are not footnotes. They are why offers fail.
4. **Compensation shape.** Base-plus-long-term versus carry. Do not anchor on a blog range. Ask for the policy.
5. **Exit.** How do people leave this institution, and where do they go? If nobody can name three alumni, ask why.
