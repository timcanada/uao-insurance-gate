# Canada’s pension system: CPP is C$793.3 billion, not $531.3 billion from 2023

Canada has a public pillar and large provincial books. That architecture is real. The live dollar table is not. The live page, dated 3 July 2026, still assigns [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) **assets exceeding $500 billion as of 2023** and then **$531.3 billion** from a 2023 annual report, files [OTPP](/ontario-teachers-pension-plan-explained/) and [OMERS](/omers-explained/) as a combined **$300 billion**, reprints OTPP at **$236.9 billion / $237 billion** and OMERS at **approximately $110 billion**, [CDPQ](/cdpq-explained/) at **$370 billion**, [AIMCo](/aimco-alberta-investment-management-corporation/) at **$160 billion**, [BCI](/bci-british-columbia-investment-management/) at **$210+ billion**, and a stacked **$1.2 trillion**. It also points at [GPIF](/gpif-japan-explained/) as a **$2 trillion** pension giant. Those leftovers are not the current filings.

## What the page can say

- **Two layers.** CPP is the national contributory plan. OTPP, OMERS, CDPQ, AIMCo, and BCI are different legal persons with different sponsors.
- **How to print scale.** Quote each filing in its own currency and date. A page that needs **$531.3 billion** from 2023 is a leftover page.
- **What they are not.** CPP, OTPP, OMERS, and CDPQ are not sovereign wealth funds.
