# Family office vs institutional investor: quote the filing, not a $10 billion band

A family office answers to a family. An institutional investor answers to members, a statute, or a board that is not the family. That contrast is real. The live dollar table is not. The live page still assigns [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) **USD 469 billion as of June 2024**, invents a peer family office of **USD 10 billion**, prints GPFG as **USD 1.3 trillion**, Harvard as **USD 51.9 billion as of June 2024**, CalSTRS as **USD 295 billion**, USS as **USD 83 billion**, and then files Berkshire Hathaway as a **USD 1.1 trillion** mega-family office. Those are leftovers and category errors. They are not the filings.

## What the comparison can say

- **Who is owed.** A public pension owes dated benefits. An endowment spends a policy rate to support a university. A [SWF](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) answers to a state. A [family office](/what-is-a-family-office/) answers to a family. Those are legal and operating forms.
- **Disclosure.** CalPERS, CalSTRS, USS, and Harvard file an official size. GPFG files in kroner. Family offices generally file nothing. See [`/largest-family-offices-in-the-world/`](/largest-family-offices-in-the-world/).
- **What is not a family office.** Berkshire Hathaway is a listed conglomerate. Mubadala is a sovereign investor. Do not file either as a family office so a “mega-FO” row has a number.
