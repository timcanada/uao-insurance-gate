# What is capital introduction for an asset owner?

Capital introduction is a networking door a prime broker opens between a manager and an allocator — an introduction, not a hired raise. A registered broker-dealer’s own public guide already lists capital introduction as a prime-brokerage service, primarily for hedge-fund clients, and lists placement-agent work as a separate investment-banking job paid by the issuer. Universal Asset Owners maps compensated finders on the [placement-agent directory](/directory/placement-agents/). This page is not fundraising advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second placement-agent definition.** That keeper is `/how-do-pensions-use-placement-agents/`. Do not mint `/cap-intro-explained/`, `/what-is-cap-intro/`, or `/capital-introduction-prime-broker/`. Do not invent a house AUM for a manager or a plan.

## Two jobs a broker already separates

J.P. Morgan Securities LLC’s public *Guide to Investment Banking Services and Prime Brokerage Services* states that JPMS is a **registered broker-dealer and investment adviser with the SEC**, and a **FINRA, MSRB, and SIPC** member. It then splits the house:

- **Prime brokerage** includes clearing, custody and settlement, securities lending, financing, and **capital introduction and business consulting services, primarily to hedge-fund clients**
- **Investment banking** includes serving as an initial purchaser, **placement agent**, or broker on a private placement; serving as **underwriter** on a registered offering; and **M&A advisory**

When that firm acts as underwriter, initial purchaser, or placement agent for an issuer, the same guide says it does **not** serve as the investor’s agent, fiduciary, or financial advisor unless expressly agreed or required by law. The investor is expected to run independent diligence.

That is the owner’s test. Cap intro is a meeting. A placement agent is a compensated finder on someone else’s raise. California already defines that second job: Cal. Code Regs. Tit. 2, § 559 (filed **28 January 2011**) — a person who acts **for compensation** as a finder, solicitor, marketer, consultant, broker, or other intermediary in connection with an offer or sale to CalPERS. How a pension files that sits on [how pensions use placement agents](/how-do-pensions-use-placement-agents/).

What an investment bank does on an issuance or an M&A file is [a different page](/what-does-an-investment-bank-do-for-a-pension-fund/).

## Questions an allocator should minute

1. Who pays the person who asked for this meeting?
2. Is this an introduction, or a raise the manager hired someone to run?
3. Will the introducer stay in the room after the first call?
4. What will they refuse to put in writing?

Do not collapse a family-office coffee into a CalPERS disclosure form. Do not skip the form when the finder is paid on the commitment.
