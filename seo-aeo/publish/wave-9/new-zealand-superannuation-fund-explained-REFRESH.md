# New Zealand Superannuation Fund (NZ Super), explained

The New Zealand Superannuation Fund is a sovereign pre-fund for future New Zealand Superannuation, managed by the Guardians of New Zealand Superannuation. The audited fund size was **NZ$85.05 billion at 30 June 2025**. A 29 April 2026 stakeholder update put the fund at **NZ$86.6 billion at 31 March 2026**. The Guardians measure themselves against a reference portfolio (80% equities / 20% fixed interest) and a Treasury-bill opportunity-cost test. This is not investment advice.

The performance page is **provisional and unaudited**. It prints a 12-month *change* in net asset value (NZ$11.37 billion to 31 May 2026). That is not a licence to invent a May 2026 NAV. Use the March 2026 **NZ$86.6 billion** until the Guardians publish a later spot size.

## What it is

Parliament created the Guardians under the New Zealand Superannuation and Retirement Income Act **2001**. The Fund began investing in **September 2003**. The job is to help pay the future cost of universal New Zealand Superannuation — a sovereign *pre-fund*, not a member-owned pension and not a KiwiSaver product.

The Guardians are an autonomous Crown entity. They invest globally. They pay New Zealand tax. The 29 April 2026 update said a **NZ$1.6 billion** tax payment in the first week of April made the Fund again the nation’s largest single taxpayer, and that tax paid now exceeds the Government’s required capital contribution.

CEO **Jo Townsend**. Co-Chief Investment Officers named on 2026 Guardians pages: **Brad Dunstan** and **Will Goodwin**. Head of Asset Allocation from 7 August 2026: **Alex Bacchus**. Do not invent a single “CIO of NZ Super” title.

## How they keep score (this is the TPA lesson)

NZ Super is one of the houses that taught the industry the [total portfolio approach](/total-portfolio-approach/) / [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/) habit:

- A simple **80/20** listed reference portfolio is the passive alternative.
- Active risk has to beat that reference, after costs, before NZ tax.
- A separate test: beat 90-day Treasury bills (the cost of government borrowing). The performance page also prints a long-term return expectation over a 20-year moving average; treat that line as the Guardians’ own “we expect,” and do not invent a UAO target.

That is why a GP pitch that only beats a private-equity peer set is incomplete here. The question is what the sleeve does to the *whole fund* versus 80/20.

## How a peer should read it

- **Prefund, not payout (yet).** Accumulation now; Treasury has discussed first drawdowns later this decade.
- **Tax is part of the mandate.** A Fund that is the largest corporate taxpayer is not a tax-exempt US public pension.
- **Reference portfolio is public.** If you cannot explain yourself against 80/20, you are not speaking this IC’s language.
- **Not Australia’s Future Fund.** Different country, different Act. See [Future Fund](/future-fund-australia-explained/).
