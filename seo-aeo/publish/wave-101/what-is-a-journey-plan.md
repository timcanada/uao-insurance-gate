# What is a UK pension journey plan?

A UK defined-benefit journey plan is the trustees’ bridge from today’s funding position to a low-dependency target by a named relevant date. The Pensions Regulator’s DB funding code (valuations on and after 22 September 2024) splits it in two: a funding journey plan that is formally part of the funding and investment strategy and needs employer agreement, and a concurrent investment journey plan for the notional allocation. It is not LDI, not a bulk annuity, and not a de-risking slogan. This page is not a ranking and not funding advice.

## Two plans, one relevant date

The Pensions Regulator’s [journey planning module](https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/document-library/code-of-practice/funding-and-investment/funding-defined-benefits/long-term-planning/journey-planning) (DB funding code of practice; activities related to valuations with effective dates on and after **22 September 2024**) is the sourced definition:

1. For schemes that have not yet reached the relevant date, trustees must plan how the scheme will reach a position of being at least **100 percent funded** on a **low dependency** funding basis on and after that date. That bridge is the **funding journey plan**. It sits inside the funding and investment strategy and is subject to **employer agreement**.
2. Trustees should also write a concurrent **investment journey plan** — how the **notional** investment allocation evolves toward the low-dependency allocation that supports those assumptions. Only the funding element is formally part of the strategy. Actual and notional allocations may differ; trustees still invest in members’ best interests.
3. Risk along the journey must be **supportable**. More risk is allowed when the employer covenant (cash flows and contingent assets) can support it, and — subject to that — when the scheme is further from the relevant date. The code splits the clock into a **reliability period** (cash flows the trustees can reasonably count on) and a **post-reliability period** out to the relevant date.

TPR’s [DB scheme funding page](https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/trustees/investment-and-db-scheme-funding) repeats the same four FIS contents: long-term objective, low-dependency target at the relevant date, investments intended at that date, and the funding journey plan. The [Annual Funding Statement 2025](https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/document-library/statements/annual-funding-statement-2025) is TPR’s own vintage: **76 percent** of schemes already fully funded on a TPR-derived low-dependency basis. That is a regulator print, not a house AUM.

LDI is the hedge. A bulk annuity is the insurance close. De-risking is the umbrella slogan. This page is the **named date and the two plans**. Do not flatten them.

## What an owner is actually buying

A corporate sponsor agreeing a journey plan is buying a **written path the regulator can read** — assumptions, notional allocation, and a covenant that can still pay if a one-in-six stress hits in the reliability period.
