# The Santiago Principles, explained

The Santiago Principles are **24** voluntary Generally Accepted Principles and Practices for sovereign wealth funds, written in **2008** by the **26** founding members of what became the International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds. They cover governance, investment and risk management. They are **subordinate to local law**. IFSWF full members undertake to implement them and to publish a self-assessment, revised at least every three years. This is not legal advice.

## What they are

IFSWF’s own page:

- Written in **October 2008** in Santiago.
- **24** Generally Accepted Principles and Practices — usually **GAPP 1–24**.
- Purpose one: improve the understanding of SWFs as professional, independent, financially oriented investors, at home and in recipient countries.
- Purpose two: encourage appropriate disclosure and a more open dialogue about what the funds actually do.
- **Principle-based, not rules-based.** Funds are diverse; each implements the Principles in its own legal wrapper. Processes are **not necessarily comparable**.
- **Voluntary.** Full members undertake to implement them. They remain **subordinate to local law**.
- Trademarked by IFSWF in **2018**.

They are not a statute, not an IMF inspection, and not a credit rating. A fund can be an IFSWF member and still be a poor disclosure case. A fund can sit outside IFSWF and still publish a clean annual report. Membership is a **signal**, not a proof.

## Who keeps them

The **International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds**, a member-owned non-profit incorporated in London. Chief Executive named on the Principles page: **Duncan Bonfield**.

Since **2016**, the Secretariat has asked members for a **self-assessment** against the Principles. IFSWF now says **all full members** publish that assessment on [ifswf.org/assessments](https://www.ifswf.org/assessments). The ask is **triennial**, though some members revise more often. The guidelines were written with the Fletcher School at Tufts.

Do not invent a 2026 member count. IFSWF’s [member list](https://www.ifswf.org/our-members) is the live register. Associate members (usually for up to two years) are institutions being established, or governments building a fund.

GAPP **24** is the “regular review” hook those self-assessments sit on.

## What they are for — and what they are not

IFSWF says the Principles have become a definitional tool in **trade, investment regulation, tax treatment and financial-market transactions**, and a framework for funds to talk to domestic stakeholders and private-sector partners.

They do **not**:

- bind a fund in court
- replace a national audit office
- tell you AUM
- make [ADIA](/adia-abu-dhabi-investment-authority/) publish a NAV
- make [GIC](/gic-singapore-explained/) publish AUM
- settle whether [Temasek](/is-temasek-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/) is a SWF

For a CIO reading a GP memo that says “Santiago-compliant,” the next question is: **where is the dated self-assessment, and which GAPP did they actually discuss?**
