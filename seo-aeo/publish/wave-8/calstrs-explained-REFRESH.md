# CalSTRS, explained

The California State Teachers’ Retirement System is the largest educator-only pension fund in the world. The Teachers’ Retirement Fund was valued at **$415.4 billion at 30 June 2026**. The FY2025–26 net investment return was **13.9%**. The latest published funded status for the Defined Benefit Program is **79.3% at 30 June 2025** — not a 2026 ratio. CalSTRS’ 2014 funding plan still targets full funding by 2046. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/calstrs-explained/`

The live page this overwrites already had the $415.4 billion year-end print. It still said funded status was **75.9% at 30 June 2023**. That line is stale. CalSTRS’ own 4 August 2026 return release and its funded-status note both print **79.3% at 30 June 2025**. The next actuarial valuation — the one that will fold in the 13.9% year — is scheduled for **May 2027**. Do not invent a 30 June 2026 funded ratio.

CalSTRS’ monthly investment-portfolio page can print a different dollar figure between fiscal year-ends. Use the 30 June 2026 **$415.4 billion** as the year-end headline until CalSTRS replaces it.

Among US publics, [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) is larger (PERF **$637.1 billion** at 30 June 2026, preliminary). CalSTRS is the larger *educator-only* book. Do not call it the largest pension on earth — that is [GPIF](/gpif-japan-explained/).

## Funding path

The 2014 funding plan raised member, employer, and state contribution rates with an explicit target of full funding by **2046**. CalSTRS says it is ahead of that schedule. The funded ratio has risen from **62.6% to 79.3%** since 2017 — eight consecutive annual increases on the published valuations. The 30 June 2025 print (79.3%) was above the 70.2% the 2014 plan had projected for that date.

CEO **Cassandra Lichnock** and CIO **Scott Chan** are named on the 4 August 2026 release. Covered service is **not** eligible for Social Security. Members who retired in FY2024–25 had, on CalSTRS’ average, 24.9 years of service and a monthly benefit of $5,740.

## How a peer should read it

- **Funded status lags returns.** A 13.9% year does not become a 2026 funded ratio until May 2027. Anyone quoting 75.9% (2023) or inventing an 85% 2026 figure is not reading CalSTRS.
- **Educator-only, no Social Security offset.** The liability is the job. The Collaborative Model (more internal management and co-investing) is a fee decision at this scale, not a personality.
- **Second US public, not a SWF.** Rank it with [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/), [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/), and [OTPP](/ontario-teachers-pension-plan-explained/) — not with [PIF](/public-investment-fund-pif/).
