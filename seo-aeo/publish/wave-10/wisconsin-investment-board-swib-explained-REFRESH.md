# Wisconsin Investment Board (SWIB), explained

The State of Wisconsin Investment Board is the independent agency that invests the Wisconsin Retirement System, the State Investment Fund, and other state trusts. Official total assets were **$178.175 billion at 31 December 2025** — not $215 billion. WRS assets were **$154.652 billion**. The Core Fund returned **14.4%** net in 2025 against a **6.8%** long-term target. SWIB serves more than **703,000** WRS participants. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/wisconsin-investment-board-swib-explained/`

The live page this overwrites still calls SWIB a **$215 billion** pension fund for **625,000** members with an **8%** assumed return. Official SWIB papers put total AUM about **$37 billion lower**, membership about **78,000 higher**, and the Core target at **6.8%**. The short twins `/wisconsin-investment-board-swib/` and `/wisconsin-swib/` are also 200 — 301 both here after this overwrite. Do not mint a fourth URL.

## Core and Variable score

From the 2026 Goals, Strategies & Performance report (net, to 31 December 2025):

| Fund | 1-year | 5-year | 10-year | 20-year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | **14.4%** | **7.1%** | **8.9%** | **7.2%** |
| Variable | **22.0%** | **11.8%** | **12.7%** | **9.3%** |

The Core’s long-term WRS investment target is **6.8%**. The 5-, 10- and 20-year Core prints are above that target. The live page’s **8%** assumption is the wrong number.

SWIB also says twenty years of active management added about **$10 billion** to the Core versus a passive 60% global equities / 40% domestic bonds book, and that five-year staff outperformance was about **$3.8 billion** net of costs. Those are SWIB’s own score-keeping lines, not a UAO estimate.

Executive Director / Chief Investment Officer: **Edwin Denson**. Board of Trustees Chair named on the 2026 compensation release: **Clyde Tinnen**.

## What it is

SWIB is an **independent state agency (1951)**, not “the pension fund.” The pension is the **WRS** — a risk-sharing defined-benefit hybrid. SWIB also runs the **State Investment Fund** (cash for state and local units) and smaller separately managed trusts. Calling the whole stack “a $215 billion pension fund” is how the live page overshot.

Statute s. 25.15(2) is the prudent-expert / diversify / sole-purpose test. The live page’s “Chapter 24” cite is the wrong chapter to lean on.

The WRS is the US public plan SWIB wants mentioned as **fully funded**. Do not invent a 2026 funded ratio SWIB did not print in these documents.

## How a peer should read it

- **$215 billion overstates the filing.** Official total is **$178.175 billion**. WRS alone is **$154.652 billion**.
- **Two WRS funds.** Core is the diversified default. Variable is optional equity. Quote the right one.
- **6.8%, not 8%.** If a consultant deck still uses 8%, it is not reading SWIB.
