# GIC vs CPP Investments: no AUM on one side, Canadian dollars on the other

GIC Private Limited does **not** publish AUM. CPP Investments printed **C$793.3 billion** at **31 March 2026**. The live comparison, dated 7 July 2026, still treats GIC as **~$936 billion** so the two books can be ranked. That dollar is not a GIC filing. CPP is a **pension**. GIC is **not**.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## What the comparison can say without inventing a pile

GIC invests a slice of Singapore’s reserves under the Ministry of Finance’s reserves framework, alongside MAS and [Temasek](/is-temasek-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/). Singapore’s MOF is explicit: GIC **manages**; it does not own the assets on its own balance sheet the way Temasek does. The legal name is **GIC Private Limited**, not “Government of Singapore Investment Corporation.”

CPP Investments is an independent Crown corporation under the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board Act. It invests Canada Pension Plan cash for contributors and beneficiaries. It is **not** a sovereign wealth fund. That Q&A already lives on [`/is-cpp-investments-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/`](/is-cpp-investments-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/).

A writer who assigns GIC **$936 billion** so the two books can be ranked is not quoting GIC.

## What is not official

- GIC as **$936 billion**, **$875 billion**, **$796 billion**, **$688 billion**, or **$1 trillion**.
- A homemade USD conversion of CPP’s **C$793.3 billion** used as a rank.
- Calling CPP a sovereign wealth fund.
- “Government of Singapore Investment Corporation” as the current legal name.
