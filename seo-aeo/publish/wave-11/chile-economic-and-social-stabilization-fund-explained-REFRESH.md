# Chile Economic and Social Stabilization Fund, explained

Chile’s Economic and Social Stabilization Fund is a fiscal stabilization reserve, not a globally diversified savings SWF. The Ministry of Finance printed a market value of **US$3,884.04 million at the end of June 2026**. Since inception the Fund has taken in **US$27,763.41 million** and paid out **US$28,292.81 million**; net investments have added **US$4,413.44 million**. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

The ESSF is an **extra source of financing for the Central Government**. Hacienda’s own uses: stabilize public finances when the cycle or an extraordinary event hits; finance fiscal deficits; make the annual contribution to the **Pension Reserve Fund**; amortize public debt.

Inflows, under the Fiscal Responsibility Law as Hacienda describes them: the positive balance after the effective fiscal surplus, minus contributions to the Pension Reserve Fund and the Central Bank, minus public-debt payments and prior-year advances. That is a **budget rule**, not a CIO’s AUM target.

Do not repeat the live page’s “copper is ~50% of government income” or “1% of GDP structural surplus” lines. Those are not on the Hacienda pages cited here.

Compare this fund with [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) only after you have respected the difference: Chile’s ESSF is a **drawdown reserve that has already paid out more than it has taken in**. Norway’s GPFG is an accumulation book.

Chile also runs a separate **Pension Reserve Fund**. Do not mash the two into one “Chile SWF” AUM.

## How a peer should read it

- **$10.8 billion is dead.** Anyone still quoting Q4 2023 is teaching a pre-drawdown stock.
- **Withdrawals exceed contributions.** The Fund is smaller than its own history. That is the point of a stabilization reserve.
- **Not a savings SWF.** If your league table needs a large, growing AUM, this is the wrong column.
- **Hacienda publishes monthly.** Use the Ministry’s market-value page, not a tracker.
