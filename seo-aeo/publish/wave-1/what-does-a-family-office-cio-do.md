# What does a family-office CIO do?

A family-office chief investment officer is accountable to a principal or a family board for the whole book — public markets, private deals, cash, and sometimes the operating companies — without the public-pension apparatus of legislation and beneficiary politics. The job is asset-owner work: set the objective, live with the residual risk, and say no. It is not a private-bank relationship manager with a grander title.

## How it differs from a pension CIO

A public-plan CIO defends a process. A family-office CIO defends a family's future and, often, a name. Liquidity can be more flexible. Governance can be thinner. Conflicts can be closer — a cousin, an operating business, a yacht that is also an asset.

Some families do not have a CIO. They have an [OCIO](/what-is-an-ocio/). That is a different contract: you have rented judgment.

## What “good” looks like on the public record

You will rarely see a 13F that tells the whole story. Judge what *is* public: speeches, rare interviews, charitable boards, and the operating companies the family still owns. Do not invent a biography.
