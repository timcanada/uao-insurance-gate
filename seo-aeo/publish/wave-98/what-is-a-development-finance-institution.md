# What is a development finance institution?

A development finance institution is a government-owned investor whose job is development — mobilising private capital in developing markets — not a sovereign wealth fund. IFSWF’s Santiago definition of a SWF is government-owned, invested for financial objectives, and includes foreign financial assets. The U.S. International Development Finance Corporation officially calls itself the U.S. government’s development finance institution. Those are different mandates. This page is not a ranking and not investment advice.

## What the official files already say

The International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds publishes the definition the funds wrote in **2008** in Appendix I of the [Santiago Principles](https://www.ifswf.org/santiago-principles). IFSWF’s [What is a Sovereign Wealth Fund?](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/) page restates three characteristics:

1. Owned by the **general government** (central or sub-national).
2. Includes investments in **foreign financial assets**.
3. Invests for **financial objectives**.

That page **excludes** public pension funds (owned by the underlying policy holders) and **central-bank reserve assets** (not invested as a SWF).

A DFI is a different government file. The [U.S. International Development Finance Corporation](https://www.dfc.gov/who-we-are/overview) says it is the **U.S. Government’s development finance institution**. Three facts from that official overview:

1. The Better Utilization of Investments Leading to Development (**BUILD**) Act was signed on **5 October 2018**.
2. DFC was **established in 2019** and combined the Overseas Private Investment Corporation with USAID’s Development Credit Authority.
3. It **partners with the private sector** to finance solutions to challenges in the **developing world**.

A SWF can have a development *sleeve*. That does not make the house a DFI. A DFI can earn a return. That does not make it a SWF. Name the statute and the mandate. Do not stack them.
