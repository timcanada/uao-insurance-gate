# The Reference Portfolio, explained

A reference portfolio is a **cheap, passive mix of public equities and government bonds** that a fund could have held instead of its real book. It sets the board’s market-risk appetite and is the hurdle every active and private-market decision has to beat, net of costs. [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/)’ live mix, from **1 July 2026**, is **75% global equities and 25% US Treasury bonds**. [NZ Super](/new-zealand-superannuation-fund-explained/)’s is **80% equities and 20% fixed interest**, **100% hedged to the New Zealand dollar**. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

Two jobs, one mix:

1. **Risk appetite.** The equity/bond split is the board’s statement of how much market risk the fund is willing to run.
2. **Hurdle.** The actual book — private equity, infrastructure, active managers, tilts — is judged against this cheap alternative. If complexity cannot beat the mix **net of all costs**, the complexity is not earning its keep.

It has to be **listed, passive, and cheap**. Private assets cannot sit in the reference book: they are not a “do nothing clever” alternative.

It is **not** a policy portfolio. A policy portfolio assigns target weights to many sleeves and scores staff on filling buckets. A reference portfolio specifies only the simple equity/bond risk level and lets the actual book compete for capital. That is the [total portfolio approach](/total-portfolio-approach/).

| of which (NZ Super building blocks) | **75%** global equities · **5%** NZ equities · **20%** global fixed income | Same | Same page, last updated September 2023 — still the published breakdown |
| CPP Investments | Base **85%** global public equity / **15%** Canadian federal and provincial nominal bonds; Additional **55% / 45%** | Reference Portfolios / Market Risk Targets for **FY2025–FY2027** | [Investment Statement, 2 Apr 2025](https://www.cppinvestments.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/InvestmentStatement-InvestmentPortfolio-EN-Apr022025.pdf) |

CalPERS also replaced **11** asset-class benchmarks with that single mix. The **discount rate stayed 6.8%**. TPA did not change it.

NZ Super’s own sentences: the Reference Portfolio is a **shadow or notional** portfolio of passive, low-cost, listed investments. It estimates expected returns, benchmarks value-add **net of all costs**, and sets the hurdle for active risk. From **1 July 2026** the Guardians’ published value-add expectation versus that mix is **0.9% a year** over a 20-year moving average (it had been **1.0%**). That is **their** target, not a UAO finding.

CPP still publishes those two-asset risk targets. In fiscal 2025 it moved **relative-performance** measurement to aggregated **benchmark portfolios** — public-index stand-ins for each strategy — because the old two-asset mix no longer looked like the multi-strategy book. Do not print the live page’s “historically around 65/35” as if it were current. The April 2025 Board exhibit is **85/15** and **55/45**.
