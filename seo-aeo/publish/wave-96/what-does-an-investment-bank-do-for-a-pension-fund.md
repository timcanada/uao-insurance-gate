# What does an investment bank do for a pension fund?

An investment bank works a transaction for an issuer, a seller, or a mandate — underwriting, a private placement, or M&A advice — and is usually paid by that side. A registered broker-dealer’s public guide already says that when it acts as underwriter or placement agent it is not the investor’s fiduciary unless the papers say so. That is a different job from a placement agent on a GP raise and from capital introduction. This page is not transaction advice.

## What a broker-dealer already lists as investment banking

J.P. Morgan Securities LLC’s public *Guide to Investment Banking Services and Prime Brokerage Services* states that JPMS is an **SEC-registered broker-dealer and investment adviser** and a **FINRA, MSRB, and SIPC** member. Part Two of that guide names the IB jobs:

- Serving as an **initial purchaser, placement agent, or broker** on a private placement (including offerings that rely on Rule 144A, Section 4(a)(2), or Regulation D)
- Serving as **underwriter** on a registered offering (IPOs, follow-ons, convertibles, bonds)
- **M&A advisory**: mergers, sales, acquisitions, LBOs, joint ventures, takeover defenses, spin-offs, divestitures, valuation and fairness opinions

Fees on offerings are negotiated deal by deal. The guide says the bank is **paid by the issuer, financial sponsor, or selling shareholder**, often as a percentage of proceeds or as discounts and commissions. On a private placement, the placement-agent fee sits in the **seller’s or issuer’s** engagement letter.

The same guide is explicit about the other side of the table: when the bank participates as underwriter, initial purchaser, or placement agent for an issuer, it does **not** serve as the investor’s agent, fiduciary, or financial advisor unless expressly agreed or required by law. The investor is expected to run independent diligence and monitor its own positions.

That is why a pension minute should name **who hired the bank**. A sovereign or family office buying a slice of the same deal is in the same seat.

## What this is not

- A [placement agent](/how-do-pensions-use-placement-agents/) hired by a GP to sell a fund to CalPERS — Cal. Code Regs. Tit. 2, § 559 (filed **28 January 2011**)
- [Capital introduction](/what-is-a-capital-introduction/) inside a prime-brokerage relationship
- [Co-investment](/what-is-co-investment-in-private-equity/) alongside a GP
- Custody, administration, or an OCIO office

FINRA Rule 5130 and 5131, cited in that same guide, can restrict who may buy certain new issues. Do not invent a waiver.
