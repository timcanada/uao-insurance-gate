# Is Temasek a sovereign wealth fund?

Temasek is a commercial investment company that **owns the assets on its own balance sheet**. The Government of Singapore is the **sole equity shareholder**. It is not the manager of official reserves — that job is [GIC](/gic-singapore-explained/)’s, with MAS holding the monetary-policy book. As at 31 March 2026 Temasek’s mark-to-market net portfolio value was **S$518 billion (US$401 billion)**. Commercial trackers often file it next to sovereign wealth funds; Temasek’s own language is investment company. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/is-temasek-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/`

The live page this overwrites still said Temasek’s portfolio was **SGD 413 billion (~USD 310 billion) at end-2024**, called the Government a “majority” owner, and printed an unsourced “8% real” ten-year target. All three are wrong or unverified. Official NPV is **S$518 billion** at 31 March 2026. Official 20-year TSR is **6.8%**; 10-year TSR is **7.1%**. MOF’s reserves page is the ownership line.

## The one-sentence legal distinction

The Ministry of Finance’s reserves page (updated 4 March 2026) is the cleanest official split:

- The Government’s assets are **mainly managed by GIC**.
- The Government is the **sole equity shareholder of Temasek**.
- Temasek **owns** what sits on Temasek’s balance sheet.

That is why [Temasek vs GIC](/temasek-vs-gic-difference/) is not a synonym quiz. One house is a fund manager of assets the state still owns. The other is a company that *is* the owner.

Temasek’s 8 July 2026 release calls Temasek “a **global investment company** headquartered in Singapore.” It does not call itself a sovereign wealth fund in that text.

## What the SWF definition actually tests

The [International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund) uses the 2008 Santiago definition: owned by general government; invests for financial objectives; includes foreign financial assets. That definition **excludes** ordinary central-bank reserves and public pension funds owned by the underlying members.

Temasek is government-owned and invests for financial return, including abroad. On a loose reading, a tracker can put it in a SWF league table. On Singapore’s own map it is **not** the reserve manager and **not** MAS. If the question is “does Temasek manage Singapore’s official reserves?” the answer is **no**. If the question is “do commercial SWF league tables include Temasek?” the answer is often **yes** — and you should say so as a classification, not as a Temasek filing.

See [what is a sovereign wealth fund?](/sovereign-wealth-fund/).

## Three pillars, not one “Singapore SWF”

Singapore’s reserve system is **MAS + GIC + Temasek**.

| | Job | Owns the assets? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MAS** | Official foreign reserves for the exchange-rate regime | The state, as reserves |
| **GIC** | Long-horizon sleeve of government assets | The Government; GIC manages |
| **Temasek** | Commercial investment company | **Temasek** |

GIC does **not** publish AUM. Its official score is the rolling 20-year real return (**3.4%** to 31 March 2026; 5.6% USD nominal). Do not add a tracker GIC AUM to Temasek’s NPV and call the sum “Singapore’s sovereign wealth.”

## How a counterparty should use the label

- Pitching a Singapore *reserve* ticket → you are probably talking to **GIC** or a MAS-adjacent process, not Temasek.
- Pitching a control process, a TPC, or a commercial co-invest → you are talking to a **company**, with a board, a published Review, and assets on its own balance sheet.
- Putting Temasek in a SWF slide deck → label it as a **tracker classification**, and put the MOF sentence underneath.
