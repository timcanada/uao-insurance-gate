# Joining an investment committee — what the seat actually is

Boarding an investment committee is not a prestige appointment. You are joining a mandate split: what staff may do without a vote, what the board must minute, and what you will refuse to put in the pack. This Careers Intelligence briefing sits beside Universal Asset Owners' [investment-committee governance](/investment-committee-governance/) page. It is not legal, fiduciary, or compensation advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second governance explainer.** Do not mint `/what-is-an-investment-committee/` or `/investment-committee-explained/`. Do not invent a house AUM to decorate a committee bio.

## The seat is a fiduciary file

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) hold capital for their own account. The [fiduciary duty](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/) stays with the house and the beneficiaries — not with the guest who flew in for the quarter.

The governance job is already written: an investment committee is a mandate split between the board and staff. It is not a second AUM. If you cannot say where advice ends and a vote begins, you do not have a seat. You have a lunch.

A [pension fund](/what-is-a-pension-fund/) committee is a public process. A sovereign or strategic fund committee is often quieter and still has a file. A family-office committee is a confidentiality problem first. Do not arrive with the wrong room’s manners.

## What to pressure-test before you accept

1. **Decision rights.** What can staff do without you, and what requires the minute?
2. **Conflicts.** Which managers, GPs, or employers still pay you?
3. **The pack.** What will you refuse to let staff put in front of the board?
4. **Attendance.** Are you a working member, or a name on the letterhead?
5. **Exit.** How does a member leave, and what still sits in the public file?
