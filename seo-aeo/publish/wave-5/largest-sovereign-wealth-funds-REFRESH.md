# The World's Largest Sovereign Wealth Funds

The largest sovereign wealth fund that publishes a current official value is Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global: **22,683 billion kroner at 30 June 2026**, managed by Norges Bank Investment Management. China’s CIC last reported **US$1.57 trillion of total assets and US$1.37 trillion of net assets at 31 December 2024**. Several of the other giants — ADIA, KIA, QIA, GIC, SAFE — do not publish official AUM, so a single dollar ranking is an estimate. Industry reports put the universe near **US$15.1 trillion in April 2026**. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/largest-sovereign-wealth-funds/`

This page is the league table. For the definition, use [what is a sovereign wealth fund](/sovereign-wealth-fund/). For Japan’s GPIF, use [largest pension funds](/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/) — it is a public pension, not a SWF.

## How to read this table

A SWF ranking looks like a scoreboard. It is not.

1. **Some of the largest books do not publish a size.** ADIA, KIA, QIA, GIC and SAFE sit in every tracker’s top ten. They do not give UAO an official AUM to reprint. We will not invent one.
2. **The numbers that do exist are not the same object.** Norway publishes a *fund value* in kroner. CIC publishes *total assets* and *net assets* in US dollars. Temasek publishes *mark-to-market net portfolio value* and owns the assets on its own balance sheet. PIF publishes *assets under management* and, separately, IFRS consolidated assets of subsidiaries. Adding those rows is a category error.
3. **Dates and FX move the order.** NBIM’s half-year figure is 30 June 2026. CIC’s last annual report is year-end 2024. A strong dollar can reshuffle a yen or krone book without a single trade.
4. **Scope is a choice.** The [IFSWF / Santiago](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund) tests are: government-owned, financial objectives, including foreign financial assets. That excludes ordinary central-bank reserves held only for monetary policy, ordinary SOEs, and member-owned public pensions. Trackers do not all draw the line in the same place.

Use the table for **order of magnitude and who discloses**. Do not take a rank into an IC memo without the source, the as-of date, and the unit.

## Giants that do not publish AUM

Print this list as often as the table. It is the reason every public ranking disagrees.

| Institution | What they *do* publish | What they do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA) | Mandate, some review language, governance | Official AUM |
| Kuwait Investment Authority (KIA) | History (1953), mandate | Official AUM |
| Qatar Investment Authority (QIA) | Activity, selected stakes | Official AUM |
| GIC, Singapore | Rolling 20-year **real** return: **3.4%** (5.6% USD nominal) to 31 Mar 2026; geography Americas 53 / APAC 22 / EMEA 19 / global 6 | AUM. See [Temasek vs GIC](/temasek-vs-gic-difference/). |
| SAFE Investment Company, China | Almost nothing at vehicle level | Official SWF AUM. SAFE is the FX-reserve system; treat tracker sizes as estimates. |

Trackers (Global SWF, SWFI, Visual Capitalist, PipelineRoad) routinely place ADIA, KIA and SAFE in or near the trillion-dollar club and GIC just below. Those estimates are useful as *someone else’s model*. They are not a filing. UAO will not launder them into a fake official number.

## What is *not* on this list

**Japan’s Government Pension Investment Fund** is the world’s largest dedicated public pension (¥293,427.6 billion at end-December 2025). It is not a sovereign wealth fund. Rankings that put GPIF in a SWF top three are using the wrong perimeter. See [largest pension funds](/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/).

**Hong Kong’s Exchange Fund** and other reserve books sit on the SWF/reserves border. Read [reserves vs SWFs](/reserve-funds-vs-sovereign-wealth-funds/) before calling them sovereign wealth funds to win a query.

## Why the public rankings disagree

They are not lying. They are measuring different things.

- **Thinking Ahead Institute Asset Owner 100 (end-2024):** NBIM US$1.7 trillion overtook GPIF US$1.6 trillion as the world’s largest *asset owner*. SAFE third at a US$1.4 trillion estimate; CIC US$1.3 trillion. That study is a year older than NBIM’s June 2026 kroner figure.
- **IE / ICEX 2026:** GPFG about US$2.1 trillion in the April 2026 cut; CIC US$1.57 trillion (matches CIC’s own total-assets line); ADIA and KIA given tracker growth rates we will not reprint as official.
- **Visual Capitalist / Global SWF 2026 graphics:** NBIM about US$2.1 trillion, then SAFE, CIC, ADIA, PIF, KIA, GIC — a clean graphic that treats estimates and filings as one column.

None of those is “the” ranking. The useful page is the one that says **which cell is a filing**.

## Three models, not one league

Size does not tell you how the book behaves.

1. **Diversified financial saver.** Norway is the clean case: global public markets, published holdings, a fiscal spending rule, almost no private equity. ADIA is usually described in the same family — we just lack the official size.
2. **Strategic / development.** PIF is the current large example: financial return *and* Vision 2030 domestic ecosystems. Mubadala is an Abu Dhabi strategic investor that *does* publish AUM (US$385 billion at end-2025). Temasek is a commercial owner, not a reserve manager.
3. **Reserve / surplus manager.** GIC and elements of SAFE and KIA sit closer to the official-reserves system. That is why GIC publishes a 20-year real return and refuses AUM.

A trillion dollars run for purchasing-power preservation is not the same counterparty as a trillion dollars run to build a domestic industrial base.

## Geography

- **Norway** stands alone with one transparent petroleum saver.
- **The Gulf** is a *cluster*: ADIA plus Mubadala, ADQ and others in Abu Dhabi; KIA; PIF; QIA. Adding “UAE SWFs” into one number is a magazine move, not a legal one.
- **China** is two very different vehicles: CIC (publishes) and SAFE (does not).
- **Singapore** is three pillars — MAS, GIC, Temasek — not one fund. MOF assigns the capital.

Royal-family offices and other court-adjacent books are a separate owner type. See [royal family investment offices](/royal-family-investment-offices/) once that page is live.

## Why this matters for universal owners

The funds at the top of any honest table are diversified enough that they cannot sell “the system.” That is the [universal owner](/universal-owner/) problem. Their allocations move the cost of capital in private markets and the voting math in public ones. Watching the league table is watching where multi-decade capital is allowed to sit — and which governments still refuse to say how much there is.

## For investment committees

- Prefer the fund’s own audited or official line, with the as-of date, over a tracker graphic.
- Do not benchmark a Gulf strategic book against NBIM because both appear in a top-ten list.
- Governance and the withdrawal rule tell you more than rank. Start with the [Santiago Principles](/santiago-principles/).
- If a banker sends a one-column “2026 ranking,” ask which cells are filings.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the largest sovereign wealth fund?**  
Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global, on every serious current table that uses official disclosures. NBIM reported a fund value of **22,683 billion kroner at 30 June 2026**.

**How big are sovereign wealth funds in total?**  
IE University and ICEX put **109 funds at US$15.1 trillion in April 2026**. Treat any total as approximate: unpublished AUM and perimeter choices move it by trillions.

**Is GIC bigger than Temasek?**  
GIC does not publish AUM, so no one outside the Government of Singapore can answer that from a filing. Temasek’s official NPV at 31 March 2026 was S$518 billion (US$401 billion). They are different legal jobs. Read [Temasek vs GIC](/temasek-vs-gic-difference/).

**Is the Public Investment Fund a top-five SWF?**  
PIF says AUM is **more than US$900 billion** in its 2025 reporting. That puts it among the largest *disclosing* funds. Tracker tables that print a precise PIF rank against unpublished ADIA/KIA figures are mixing a filing with a model.

**Why do some lists put Japan first?**  
They have included GPIF. GPIF is a pension reserve for named beneficiaries, not a SWF. Different page.
