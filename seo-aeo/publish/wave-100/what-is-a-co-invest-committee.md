# What is a co-invest committee?

A co-invest committee is the delegated approval for a single deal beside a fund — not a second definition of co-investment and not the full investment committee. CalPERS’ official Total Fund Investment Policy already writes the owner-side version: private-equity staff manage partnerships, co-investments, and customized accounts; any one general partner is capped at 15 percent of PE net committed capital unless the Committee specifically approves; any one fund is capped at 35 percent of that fund. This page is not a ranking and not a deal memo.

## Delegation is the product

CalPERS’ official [Total Fund Investment Policy](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/sites/default/files/spf/docs/total-fund-investment-policy.pdf) (Private Equity program text, also filed as the 17 June 2024 Investment Committee attachment) is the sourced owner file:

1. Staff shall manage the PE program as a whole with **specific criteria** appropriate to partnership investments, **co-investments**, and Customized Investment Accounts.
2. Aggregate net committed capital to any one general partner is limited to **15 percent or less** of PE’s total net committed capital. **Any exception must be specifically approved by the Committee.**
3. CalPERS commitments to any one fund cannot exceed **35 percent** of that fund. For delegated authority, **single-asset GP-led secondaries are treated as co-investments**. Co-investments alongside institutional investors with which the PE program does not have an active commitment are subject to further restrictions in the Private Equity Program PRP.

That is a **committee-and-delegation** design. The full board does not sit on every add-on. The Committee still owns the concentration exceptions. A GP that runs an internal “co-invest committee” to allocate the same deal among LPs is the other side of the table — allocation of an investment opportunity, not the owner’s delegated authority.

## What an owner is actually buying

A pension that stands up a co-invest lane is buying **speed with a written cap** — staff can take the deal inside the policy; the Committee sees the GP that would break 15 percent.

A placement agent selling a co-invest is selling into that delegated file, not into a second definition of co-investment. How pensions use placement agents sits on [how pensions use placement agents](/how-do-pensions-use-placement-agents/).
