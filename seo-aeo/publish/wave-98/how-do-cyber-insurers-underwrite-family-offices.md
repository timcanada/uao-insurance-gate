# How do cyber insurers underwrite family offices?

A cyber insurer underwrites a family office for a leak of the family file — network intrusion and the mishandling of electronic data — not a consumer breach blog. The NAIC already defines cybersecurity insurance as commercial coverage solely intended to manage those exposures, including personally identifiable information. Universal Asset Owners already treats a family-office leak as estate risk. This page is not a policy, not a premium quote, and not a vendor list.

**Do not treat this page as a second cybersecurity-directory overwrite or a second family-office definition.** Those keepers are `/directory/cybersecurity/` and `/what-is-a-family-office/`. ## What the insurance file already is

The NAIC Blanks (E) Working Group’s [cybersecurity-insurance reporting instructions](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/2023-05BWG_Modified.pdf) (2023-05BWG) define the product for the statutory blank. Three facts from that text:

1. **Cybersecurity insurance** is commercial coverage — a single policy or a multi-peril coverage part — **solely intended** to help manage risks from **network intrusions** and the **improper handling of electronic data**, including **personally identifiable information** and other sensitive information in electronic form.
2. The cover may be written as a **stand-alone primary** policy, as an **endorsement** to another policy, or as **excess** above a primary limit.
3. An endorsement that acts like a separate policy — **separate premium, deductible, and limit** — is recorded as if it were primary. An endorsement with no extra premium or separate limit stays on the base policy’s line.

That is the underwriting file: what data lives where, who can export the family or beneficiary file, and whether the form is written on its own paper or hung on another policy. Do not invent a limit.

[What is a family office?](/what-is-a-family-office/) already says the point of the structure is **one book** plus tax, estate, and administration. The [cybersecurity directory](/directory/cybersecurity/) already says a leak is **estate risk**, and that a bank-RFP platform can be the wrong product for a twelve-person office.

The systems that keep the book sit on [what a family-office technology stack is](/what-is-a-family-office-technology-stack/). Insurance is not the stack. It is the policy that responds when the stack fails.

## What a principal should ask before a binder

1. Is this stand-alone, an endorsement, or excess — and is the premium, deductible, and limit separate?
2. Does the form cover the family file at a vendor, not only servers the office owns?
3. Who can see the application? A leak in the underwriting file is still a leak.
4. What will you refuse to put in a claim report that a principal can read?
