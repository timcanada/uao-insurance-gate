# How do owners handle personal trading?

Personal trading is the access-person rule: holdings reports, quarterly transactions, and pre-clearance of IPOs and limited offerings. SEC Rule 204A-1 already writes that minimum for registered investment advisers. Public pensions and sovereign wealth funds are often not RIAs — do not paste the rule onto a CalPERS desk or a ministry as if it were. This page is not legal advice and not a pay scale.

**Do not treat this page as a second newsroom ethics overwrite or a second code-of-ethics definition.** Those keepers are `/ethics/` and `/what-is-a-code-of-ethics-for-an-investment-office/`. Do not mint `/personal-trading-policy/`, `/access-person-trading/`, or `/how-owners-trade-personally/`. Do not invent pay.

## What Rule 204A-1 already requires

[17 CFR § 275.204A-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.204A-1) applies to an investment adviser **registered or required to be registered** under Advisers Act § 203. The Commission adopted the rule on **2 July 2004** (69 FR 41708, **9 July 2004**). Three facts from the current text:

1. **Holdings.** An access person files a holdings report **no later than 10 days** after becoming an access person (current as of a date no more than **45 days** prior), then **at least once each 12-month period** (again current within 45 days).
2. **Transactions.** Quarterly securities-transaction reports are due **no later than 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter**.
3. **Pre-approval.** Access persons must obtain approval **before** they acquire beneficial ownership in a security in an **initial public offering** or a **limited offering**.

An **access person** is a supervised person who has nonpublic information about client trades or reportable-fund holdings, or who makes or sees nonpublic securities recommendations. If investment advice is the adviser’s primary business, directors, officers, and partners are **presumed** access persons.

Exceptions already in the rule: accounts with no influence or control; automatic investment plans; and duplicate broker confirms the adviser already holds.

That is the registered-adviser file. A public pension, a sovereign wealth fund, or a family office that is **not** an SEC adviser will have its own statute, board rule, or employment contract. Name that file. Do not import 204A-1 by analogy and call it law.

## What a newsroom already prints — and what it is not

Universal Asset Owners’ own [ethics](/ethics/) page (version **1.0**, **28 May 2026**) is a journalistic code, not an adviser code: editorial staff do not own or trade individual securities of issuers they cover; diversified funds are acceptable; a holdings disclosure goes to the Standards Editor; gifts above a notional **US$100** are declined, returned, or donated. **Do not overwrite that page.** It is not the investment-office how-to.
