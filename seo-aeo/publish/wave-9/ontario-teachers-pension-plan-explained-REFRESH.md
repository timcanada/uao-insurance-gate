# Ontario Teachers’ Pension Plan (OTPP), explained

Ontario Teachers’ Pension Plan is Canada’s largest single-profession pension. The 2025 annual report put net assets at **C$279.4 billion at 31 December 2025**, with a **6.7%** total-fund net return and a 13th year fully funded. OTPP’s investor-relations page now prints **C$303.2 billion** of net assets at **30 June 2026**. It invests globally, largely in-house, for about **346,000** working members and pensioners. This is not investment advice.

The C$303.2 billion print is OTPP’s own IR headline. It is **not** a substitute for the audited year-end report. Do not invent a first-half 2026 return; OTPP has not put a 1H 2026 percentage on that page.

Contributions and benefits in 2025, from the results release: member and employer contributions **C$4.1 billion**; benefits paid **C$8.5 billion**; administrative expenses **C$1.0 billion**. The plan is a net payer. Growth in 2025 came from investment income, not from contribution surplus.

## What it is

Ontario Teachers’ was created in **1990** as an independent joint-sponsored defined-benefit plan for Ontario’s public-school teachers. It is a core exhibit of the Canadian model: arm’s-length from government, professional board, large in-house teams, direct private-market ownership.

It is **not** [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) (the national CPP manager), **not** [CalSTRS](/calstrs-explained/) (US educator-only), **not** [CAAT](/caat-pension/) (Ontario colleges), and **not** [OPTrust](/optrust/) (OPSEU). Same profession idea as CalSTRS, different statute and different dollar. CAAT and OPTrust are different Ontario files.

## 2025 in one paragraph

OTPP said 2025 was a good outcome in a hard year that missed the 7% annual target and missed the benchmark. Gold, venture growth, and public equity helped. Private equity and real estate did not. Active programmes are supposed to beat the benchmark; 2025 did not. That sentence is OTPP’s, not a UAO verdict.

## How a peer should read it

- **Two dates, two numbers.** C$279.4bn is the audited year-end. C$303.2bn is the mid-year IR headline. Use both, labelled.
- **Fully funded is the point.** A 13th year with a surplus is why this IC can stay in private markets while some US publics cannot.
- **Net payer.** Benefits already exceed contributions. The investment book has to work.
