# The endowment model, explained

The endowment model is a **perpetual-spending** framework: take equity-like and illiquid risk because the institution can, and pay a smoothed spending rule toward the operating budget. Yale News printed fiscal 2025 at **$44.1 billion** and **11.1%** net. Harvard Management Company printed **$56.9 billion** and **11.9%** at the same **30 June 2025** date. The named-model keeper is [`/yale-endowment-model/`](/yale-endowment-model/). This page is the category. This is not investment advice.

## What the category means

Three tests. Miss one and you are copying the allocation without the institution.

1. **A perpetual spend.** Yale’s Corporation target is **5.25%** with an 80/20 smoother. Harvard’s FY2025 payout rate in the fact book is **5.0%**. A DB payroll is not this.
2. **Illiquidity you can actually hold.** Private partnerships only work if next year’s spend is a fraction of NAV, not a monthly pension cheque.
3. **Access.** Swensen’s 1985–2021 run at Yale is the origin story ([Yale News coda](https://news.yale.edu/2021/10/22/david-swensens-coda)). Copying 90% “equity-like” without the manager roster is fee drag.

The **Norwegian model** is the other template: listed, cheap, transparent. GPFG official book **22,683 billion kroner** at 30 June 2026. Different legal person, different board question.

The **total portfolio approach** is a public-pension governance model scored on one cheap mix. CalPERS’ live mix is **75/25**. Do not call TPA “the endowment model for pensions.”
