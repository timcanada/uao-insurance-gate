# Korea Investment Corporation (KIC), explained

Korea Investment Corporation is the sovereign wealth fund of the Republic of Korea. It manages public funds entrusted by the Korean government and the Bank of Korea. As of the end of December 2025, KIC reported about USD 232.0 billion of assets under management, a 13.91% total portfolio return for 2025, and cumulative investment returns of about USD 122.4 billion since inception. Headquarters are in Seoul, with five overseas offices. This is not investment advice.

**Desk:** UAO Research · **Owner type:** Sovereign wealth fund · **HQ:** Seoul

KIC describes its goal as increasing sovereign wealth and helping develop the domestic finance industry. That is a SWF mandate with a national-finance overlay — not a public pension owed to named Korean workers (that is closer to the National Pension Service).

## How a peer should read it

On global league tables KIC sits in the **hundreds of billions**, not the trillion-dollar Gulf / Norway / China cluster. It is still a first-class counterparty: official annual reporting, a published traditional/alternatives split, and a Seoul HQ that other Asians and Canadians actually visit. Do not treat it as a satellite of NPS. Do not treat NPS as a SWF.
