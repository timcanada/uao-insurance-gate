# Legal counsel for sovereign funds, pensions and family offices

Asset owners retain counsel for mandates that do not look like ordinary corporate work: fiduciary duty, fund formation, cross-border investment, sanctions, employment of senior investment staff, and disputes that sit under public or family accountability. This hub is Universal Asset Owners' map of that category — written for the desk that hires, and usable by the firms that want to be found. It is not a ranking and not legal advice.

## What owners are actually buying

A pension general counsel and a sovereign fund's legal chief are not shopping for the same memo.

Public pensions buy advice that will survive a board, a legislature, and a newspaper. The question is often fiduciary process: what was considered, who decided, what was recorded.

Sovereign and strategic funds buy advice that will survive a ministry, a sanctions screen, and a counterparty in another legal system. The Santiago Principles are a governance baseline for many funds, not a marketing line — see the [International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds](https://www.ifswf.org/santiago-principles).

Family offices buy confidentiality, succession, and the unglamorous work of holding companies, immigration, and disputes inside a family. The “firm that did the deal” is sometimes the wrong firm for the office.

If you sell into this room, say which of those jobs you do. If you buy, ask for the last three matters *like yours*, redacted.

## The questions a CIO or GC should ask

1. Who is the working partner, and have they sat in an investment committee, or only in a closing room?
2. Where is the advice privileged, and where will it be discoverable?
3. How do you handle a conflict when you also represent the manager on the other side of the table?
4. What will you refuse to put on letterhead?
