# ISIF (Ireland Strategic Investment Fund), explained

The Ireland Strategic Investment Fund is Ireland’s **sovereign development fund**, controlled and managed by the National Treasury Management Agency. It is **two books**, not a €24 billion pile. The 2025 financial statements print a Discretionary Portfolio of **€9.7 billion at 31 December 2025** (2024: **€8.9 billion**) and Directed Investments of **€6.422 billion** (2024: **€7.667 billion**). The Discretionary book returned **8.1%** in 2025. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/isif-ireland-strategic-investment-fund-explained/`

The live page this overwrites still says ISIF holds **approximately €24 billion**. NTMA’s own 2024 annual report called the Fund a **€16.6 billion** house (**€8.9 billion** Discretionary + **€7.7 billion** Directed). The €24 billion line **overstates**. There is no live short twin to 301.

Do not add 9.7 and 6.422 and invent a new “ISIF AUM” headline. NTMA printed a combined figure in 2024. The 2025 statements print the two books. Until NTMA reprints a single total, quote the two books.

Do not mash the **€9.7 billion Discretionary NAV** with the **€9.7 billion committed-to-Ireland** line. One is a stock. The other is cumulative commitments since 2014.

## The two books

**Discretionary Portfolio.** Statutory “double bottom line”: invest on a commercial basis to support economic activity and employment in Ireland. Irish Portfolio for domestic impact; Global Portfolio as a liquidity sleeve for Irish deals and directed withdrawals. Themes NTMA names: climate, housing and enabling investments, scaling indigenous businesses, food and agriculture.

**Directed Portfolio.** Assets held on **Minister for Finance** direction. This is not the CIO’s discretionary book.

What changed in 2025, from the financial statements:

- The Fund **completed the sale of the State’s directed AIB shareholding on 17 June 2025**. Closing AIB directed valuation: **nil** (2024: **€2.324 billion**).
- **€1.264 billion** of AIB sale proceeds transferred to the Exchequer.
- **€500 million** transferred to the Land Development Agency (2024: €325 million).
- Directed leftovers at year-end: Irish exchequer notes **€5.846 billion**, HBFI loan **€303 million**, cash **€235 million**, plus small receivables — the **€6.422 billion** total.

If a slide still says “ISIF owns AIB,” send it back.

The 2025 statements also disclose a **July 2025** fraudulent payment request for **€5 million**. **€2.5 million** remained unrecovered at 31 December 2025 and was recognised as a loss. That is a C&AG-era control fact, not a NAV.

## What it is

ISIF is the successor to the **National Pensions Reserve Fund** (assets transferred December 2014). It is a **sovereign development fund**, not a savings SWF and not Ireland’s new **Future Ireland Funds** (a separate NTMA business). Compare it with development mandates, not with [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/).

It is not [Ireland’s](/future-generations-fund/) rainy-day fiscal fund under another name. The Future Ireland Funds sit beside it at the NTMA. Do not add them.

Do not invent a 2025 combined “ISIF AUM.” Do not put €24 billion back on this URL.

## How a peer should read it

- **€24 billion is dead — and was too high even in 2024.** The last official combined print was **€16.6 billion**.
- **Two books.** Discretionary is the development mandate. Directed is the minister’s leftover bank-rescue and cash book, now smaller after AIB.
- **8.1% is the Discretionary return.** It is not a Directed return and not a blended house return unless NTMA prints one.
- **Commitments ≠ NAV.** €9.7 billion committed since inception is not the 2025 Discretionary stock, even though the euro figure matches.
