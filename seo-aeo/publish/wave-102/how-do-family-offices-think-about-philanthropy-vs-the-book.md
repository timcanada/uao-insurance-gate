# How do family offices think about philanthropy vs the book?

Philanthropy versus the family-office book is a two-legal-persons problem, not a spending-rate problem. Internal Revenue Code section 4941 taxes each act of self-dealing between a disqualified person and a private foundation — a sale or lease, a loan, goods or services, compensation, or a transfer to or use by a disqualified person of foundation income or assets. The initial tax is 10 percent on the self-dealer; an additional 200 percent applies if the act is not corrected. Section 4942(e) sets a private foundation’s minimum investment return at 5 percent. The SEC family-office rule (17 CFR 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1) is a different person: family clients only, exclusively controlled by family members or family entities. Mixing the foundation and the book is a 4941 problem. This page is not a ranking and not tax advice.

## Two persons, one family

Official [26 U.S.C. § 4941](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941), [26 U.S.C. § 4942](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4942), and the SEC [family-office rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.202(a)(11)(G)-1) are the sourced split:

1. **§ 4941.** There is a tax on each act of **self-dealing** between a disqualified person and a private foundation. Self-dealing includes a sale, exchange, or lease of property; a loan; furnishing goods, services, or facilities; paying compensation; and a **transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the foundation’s income or assets**. The initial tax on the self-dealer is **10 percent** of the amount involved. If the act is not corrected, an additional tax of **200 percent** applies.
2. **§ 4942(e).** A private foundation’s **minimum investment return is 5 percent** of the excess of non-charitable-use assets over acquisition indebtedness. That is a **distribution test for the foundation**. How endowments appropriate under UPMIFA already sits on [how endowments set spending rules](/how-do-endowments-set-spending-rules/). Do not reprint Yale **5.25%** here as if this were the spending page.
3. **17 CFR § 275.202(a)(11)(G)-1.** A family office has **no clients other than family clients**, is wholly owned by family clients, and is **exclusively controlled** by one or more family members and/or family entities. The family-office **book is the family’s capital**, not a 501(c)(3). A family-funded charity can sit next to that book as a family client. It is still a **separate legal person**.

University endowment versus Ford/Gates already sits on [endowment vs foundation investing](/endowment-vs-foundation-investing/). A [family constitution](/what-is-a-family-constitution/) can name both persons. It does not merge them. What a family office is already sits on [what is a family office](/what-is-a-family-office/).
