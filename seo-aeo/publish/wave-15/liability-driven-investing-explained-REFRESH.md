# Liability-driven investing (LDI), explained

Liability-driven investing builds a pension’s assets so they **move with the present value of promised benefits** — usually with long-dated government bonds and interest-rate derivatives. Leveraged LDI does the same hedge with less cash and more collateral calls. The Bank of England’s official 2022 intervention bought **£19.3 billion** of gilts between **28 September and 14 October** after LDI selling. This is **not** a [total-portfolio](/total-portfolio-approach/) or [reference-portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/) process. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

The UK Pensions Regulator’s 24 April 2023 guidance, in their words: many defined-benefit schemes use LDI “to better match their assets to their liabilities.” LDI can be leveraged or unleveraged. In leveraged LDI, schemes use instruments to increase exposure to gilts, index-linked gilts, and fixed-income derivatives, and **must post collateral**. Those collateral demands can change over short periods when interest rates change.

Two jobs, one hedge:

1. **Match duration / inflation.** When long rates fall, DB liabilities rise. The matching book is supposed to rise with them.
2. **Free capital (the leveraged version).** Derivatives and repo deliver the hedge without tying the whole fund to low-returning cash gilts — until the margin call arrives.

That is the opposite question from a [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/). LDI asks what you **owe**. TPA asks whether the whole fund beat a cheap equity/bond mix.

## A UK exhibit that is not “the LDI fund”

[USS](/uss-universities-superannuation-scheme/) is the UK’s largest private pension and a hybrid DB/DC. Official year to **31 March 2026**: DB Retirement Income Builder net assets **£79.8 billion**, estimated surplus **£15.8 billion** (125% on monitoring of the 2023 valuation), DC Investment Builder **£4.2 billion**, **599,000** members. USS is **not** a synonym for leveraged LDI. It is the kind of DB book that *uses* matching.

## What it is not

- **Not TPA.** CalPERS’ live mix is a **75/25** [reference portfolio](/reference-portfolio-explained/) from **1 July 2026**. Different country, different question.
- **Not a SWF savings rule.** [Alaska](/alaska-permanent-fund-explained/) POMV and [NZ Super](/new-zealand-superannuation-fund-explained/) 80/20 are not LDI.
- **Not a promise that the 2022 loop cannot recur.** TPR’s 2023 guidance is about **collateral buffers and resilience**, not a clean bill of health.
