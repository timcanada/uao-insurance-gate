# SOFAZ (Azerbaijan State Oil Fund), explained

The State Oil Fund of the Republic of Azerbaijan is Azerbaijan’s oil-and-gas savings and stabilization SWF. SOFAZ’s own recent-figures page printed assets of **USD 72,596.6 million at 30 June 2026**. At the start of 2026 the Fund printed **USD 73,541.5 million**; at 31 March 2026 it printed **USD 73,515.9 million**. This is not a $43 billion 2023 leftover. This is not investment advice.

Q1 asset-management revenue was **AZN 1,376.8 million**, including extra-budgetary revenues of **AZN 2,117.9 million** from gold and FX, against a **AZN 741.1 million** mark-to-market decline on the investment portfolio. That is why a flat-to-down asset stock can sit next to a large gold line.

## What it is

SOFAZ is a **state extra-budgetary fund**. It collects hydrocarbon revenues, invests them, and transfers a legislated sum to the state budget. It is closer to [Norway’s GPFG](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) in *job* — convert a depleting resource into a financial stock — than to a holding-company SWF such as [Temasek](/is-temasek-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/) or [ICD](/investment-corporation-of-dubai-icd-explained/). The transfer line is the point: this is also a **budget stabilizer**, not a lockbox that never opens.

It is an IFSWF house and sits under the [Santiago Principles](/santiago-principles/). Membership is a signal, not a NAV.

Do not invent a CEO name that is not on the page you are citing. Use oilfund.az for people moves.

Do not mash SOFAZ with SOCAR. The national oil company is a different legal person. The recent-figures page even records a completed SOFAZ financing of SOCAR’s ACG share — that is a historical project line, not an AUM.

## How a peer should read it

- **$43 billion is dead.** Anyone still quoting December 2023 is missing the 2024–26 gold and market move.
- **The budget transfer is the product.** Q1 alone sent **AZN 3.21 billion** to the state. Read the Fund as a fiscal tool with a market book, not as a closed-end savings SWF.
- **Gold is not a footnote.** At 31 March 2026 it was more than a third of the printed portfolio.
