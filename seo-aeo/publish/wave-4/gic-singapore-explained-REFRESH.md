# GIC, explained

GIC Private Limited is the professional manager of the Government of Singapore’s long-horizon assets — not the owner of them. It does not publish AUM. For the 20 years to 31 March 2026 it reported a 3.4% annualised real return (5.6% USD nominal), down from 3.8% real on the window that ended 31 March 2025. From 1 April 2026 it is moving to a Strategic Portfolio of equities, fixed income and real assets. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research

## What it is

GIC was established in 1981 to invest official reserves that had grown larger than a city-state needed in cash and short bonds. The Ministry of Finance’s current formulation (page updated 4 March 2026): GIC is “a professional fund manager that manages Government assets.” The mandate is to preserve and enhance the **international purchasing power** of those reserves.

That sentence excludes two jobs other people assign to it. It is not MAS. It is not Temasek. MAS holds Official Foreign Reserves for the exchange-rate regime. Temasek is a company that *owns* a commercial portfolio. GIC sits in the middle: diversified, mostly public markets plus a smaller alternatives book, with no domestic-control mandate.

The Government, MOF says, does not pick GIC’s individual investments. Ministers sit on the GIC board; management still runs the book. The elected President and the Auditor-General sit over the reserve-protection framework. Those are governance facts, not marketing.

## Scale

GIC does not publish assets under management. Singapore treats the stock of reserves as sensitive. Outside trackers still print a point estimate; treat every one of them as an error band. This page will not launder a tracker figure into an official AUM.

What GIC *does* publish is the return series and, this year, a geographic mix.

## The number it actually reports

The primary metric is the rolling 20-year annualised **real** return — time-weighted, so inflows and outflows do not flatter the manager.

| Window ending 31 March 2026 | USD nominal | Real (after global inflation) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20-year | 5.6% | **3.4%** |
| 10-year | 6.2% | — |
| 5-year | 3.6% | — |

Source: GIC news release, 24 July 2026, and the 2025/26 *Report on the Management of the Government’s Portfolio*.

The 20-year real print fell from 3.8% (window to 31 March 2025). That is what a rolling window does when a strong old year drops out and a more defensive new year comes in. GIC’s own commentary: the last decade split into a pre-COVID low-vol regime and a post-COVID regime of geopolitics, inflation, and technology; the house increased resilience and accepted lower headline returns.

GIC illustrates the nominal 20-year 5.6% as US$1 million in 2006 becoming about US$3 million in 2026. That is their arithmetic, not ours.

## What it holds (and what it stopped itemising)

Through 2024/25 GIC still published a point mix (equities / fixed income / real assets). In the 2025/26 report it describes the same three *groups* and says that, in the year to 31 March 2026, **equities’ share rose, fixed income’s share fell, real assets were stable**. It does not give a new 51/26/23. Do not keep last year’s percentages in the lead.

Geography, 31 March 2026 (GIC Table 3):

| Region | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Americas | 53% |
| Asia-Pacific | 22% |
| Europe, Middle East and Africa | 19% |
| Global | 6% |

The United States is the largest single market. GIC says it does not *allocate* by geography; the map is the residue of bottom-up work.

From 1 April 2026 the house is transitioning to a **Strategic Portfolio** that represents the client’s risk appetite: equities for growth, fixed income for income, real assets for inflation. The GIC Portfolio is supposed to outperform that Strategic Portfolio inside approved risk limits, with more freedom to move between public and private equity inside the equities group. CEO Lim Chow Kiat’s line in the 24 July release: judgement, diversification, and the agility to act when the ticket is good.

## How to read it next to Temasek

See [Temasek vs GIC](/temasek-vs-gic-difference/). Short version: GIC does not own the assets; Temasek does. GIC is measured on a 20-year real number and does not print AUM; Temasek prints NPV and multi-horizon TSR. GIC is the diversified reserve sleeve; Temasek is the owner of operating companies, including a large Singapore-headquartered book.

## Why other asset owners watch it

The 20-year real test is a governance device. It is how a state investor refuses a one-year beauty contest. The 2026 framework is the same institution admitting the old public-vs-private boxes were too tight for the book it actually runs. Counterparties should expect a large, quiet LP that will do real assets and private markets, will rarely want control, and will not tell you the denominator.
