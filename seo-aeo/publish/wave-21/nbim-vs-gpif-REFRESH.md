**Two official books. Two legal jobs. No homemade dollar rank.**

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

This page overwrites a live UAO compare (7 July 2026) that prints NBIM as **more than $2 trillion**, GPIF as **roughly $1.9 trillion**, and still carries the stale GPIF FY2025 return **15.83%**. Those dollar ranks are not filings.

Print kroner next to yen. Do not invent a US-dollar winner.

## What the comparison can say without inventing a pile

NBIM invests a petroleum-era savings book for the Norwegian state. The Ministry of Finance sets the mandate; NBIM takes the day-to-day decisions. GPIF invests reserves for Japan’s public pension system. The cash it exists to support is pension payments, not a sovereign savings rule.

A writer who converts both books at this morning’s FX rate and then ranks them is not quoting either house. The yen/dollar and krone/dollar crosses move; the filings do not.

The live page’s **¥2.7 trillion / $18.5 billion** annual GPIF payout line is not on the FY2025 English summary UAO will reprint. Leave it off.

## What is not official

- NBIM as **more than $2 trillion**, **$1.76 trillion**, or any other homemade dollar AUM.
- GPIF as **roughly $1.9 trillion**, or FY2025 as **15.83%**.
- Treating GPIF as a sovereign wealth fund.
- A homemade ESG-versus-liability morality play that depends on those dollars.
