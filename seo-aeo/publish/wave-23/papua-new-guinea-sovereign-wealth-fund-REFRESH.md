# Papua New Guinea’s sovereign wealth fund: an Organic-Law design, not a $2.7 billion pot

Papua New Guinea’s Department of Treasury describes a Sovereign Wealth Fund made of two designed sleeves — a **Stabilisation Fund** and a **Development Fund** — under the Organic Law on the Sovereign Wealth Fund. The house has **not** published an official AUM that this desk can cite. The live page, dated 7 July 2026, is right that PNG does **not** currently operate a funded SWF, then treats **approximately $2.7 billion** of PNG LNG government revenue in peak years 2014–2015 as if that were the missing fund. LNG project revenue is not a sovereign-wealth-fund NAV.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## Official numbers (there is no AUM)

| Line | Official print |
| --- | --- |
| Statute | Organic Law on the Sovereign Wealth Fund (2015, as cited on the Treasury SWF page and in 2026 government consultations) |
| Designed sleeves (Treasury) | **Stabilisation Fund** and **Development Fund** |
| Stabilisation withdrawals (Treasury) | Limited to the 15-year long-term moving average of mineral and petroleum revenues as a share of non-mining revenue; must go through the National Budget |
| Official AUM | **None published** |
| PNG LNG $2.7 billion (2014–2015) | Government revenue from a project, **not** SWF capital |

Treasury’s own page still reads as a design note, not a funded-portfolio report. It names two funds and a withdrawal rule. It does not print a NAV. Do not invent one.

## What this is

The Organic Law created a legal SWF framework so mineral and petroleum revenues could be stabilised and saved. Treasury’s current public page still describes that design. 2026 government consultations have been about **operationalising** the law, not about publishing a current book value. Until a Treasury, Bank of Papua New Guinea, or statutory SWF filing prints a NAV, this desk prints **no official AUM**.

## What is not official

- **$2.7 billion** as SWF AUM, current or historical. That is a live leftover from PNG LNG government revenue in 2014–2015.
- A homemade Stabilisation + Development combined pile.
- A claim that PNG “lacks the institutional infrastructure” as a substitute for the Organic Law. The law exists. The funded portfolio does not.
- Dollarizing a later unofficial estimate.
