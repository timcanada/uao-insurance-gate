# State holding company vs SWF: Temasek is S$518 billion; GPFG is kroner

A state holding company owns operating assets on its own balance sheet. A sovereign wealth fund is a mandated investment book. That contrast is real. The live dollar table is not. The live page still assigns SASAC **approximately $2.3 trillion**, GPFG **$1.36 trillion**, Temasek **$421 billion**, ADIA **$172 billion** plus a leftover **SGRF**, Kuwait **SGRF $370 billion**, IFSWF members **$16.4 trillion**, and PIF **$925 billion**. Those are tracker numbers. They are not the filings.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

Print Singapore dollars for Temasek. Print kroner for GPFG. Do **not** invent a SASAC or IFSWF pile so the contrast can be ranked.

Oman’s **SGRF** was assembled into OIA in **2020**. It is not an Abu Dhabi sibling of ADIA, and it is not Kuwait’s General Reserve Fund. Kuwait’s GRF is one of **KIA’s two books**. Do not file it as “SGRF, the world’s oldest SWF, $370 billion.”

## What the comparison can say

- **Ownership.** A holding company *is* the owner of the operating stakes on its balance sheet. [Temasek](/is-temasek-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/) is the exhibit: the Government is the sole equity shareholder; Temasek owns what it holds. A [SWF](/sovereign-wealth-fund/) such as [GPFG](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) manages a mandated book for the state. It is not a conglomerate of national champions.
- **Disclosure.** Some houses print a NAV or a fund value in their own currency. Some refuse (ADIA, KIA, QIA, SAFE, GIC). Quote the filing or say no AUM.
- **How to print scale.** Do not convert kroner or Singapore dollars into a homemade US-dollar league so a holding company can be called “smaller than a SWF.”

PIF is a [sovereign wealth fund](/public-investment-fund-pif/) with official current language **more than US$900 billion**. That does not make every state-owned operating company a SWF, and it does not make SASAC a SWF AUM you can add.
