# Universities Superannuation Scheme (USS), explained

The Universities Superannuation Scheme is the UK’s largest private pension scheme and a hybrid DB/DC plan for UK higher education. In the year to **31 March 2026** the defined-benefit Retirement Income Builder’s net assets grew to **£79.8 billion** and the estimated surplus to **£15.8 billion** (125% funded on monitoring of the 2023 valuation). The defined-contribution Investment Builder was **£4.2 billion**. Membership was **599,000**. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

USS is a hybrid scheme for UK higher-education employers.

- **Retirement Income Builder (DB).** Guaranteed, inflation-linked pension on salary up to a threshold. This is the book whose funding position drove a decade of industrial action and then the surplus.
- **Investment Builder (DC).** Salary above the threshold, plus voluntary contributions, in member-directed accounts.

The Trustee is Universities Superannuation Scheme Limited. Implementation sits with USS Investment Management Limited (USSIM). Chair of the USS Board on the 2026 release: **Dame Kate Barker**. Group Chief Executive: **Carol Young**.

## The swing that still matters

The 2020 valuation showed a large deficit (the live page’s **£14.1 billion** 2020 figure). The 2023 valuation and subsequent monitoring produced a surplus. The 2026 accounts put that surplus at **£15.8 billion**. That is a rates-and-hedging story as much as an alpha story. USS itself published a 15 July 2026 explainer on the hedging strategy. Do not reduce the swing to “equities went up.”

The DB growth portfolio returned **9.5% p.a.** over three years to 31 March 2026, ahead of the 7.7% growth-asset assumption in the 2023 valuation. The DB fund outperformed its liabilities by **9.4% p.a.** over those three years.
