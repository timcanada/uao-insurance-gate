# Brunei Investment Agency (BIA), explained

The Brunei Investment Agency is the statutory body that holds and manages Brunei’s General Reserve Fund and the government’s external assets. It was created in 1983 under the Brunei Investment Agency Act (Chapter 137). It does not publish AUM or an annual report. Outside trackers put the book near US$78 billion as of early 2026; treat that as an estimate. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research

## What the statute actually says

The Attorney General’s Chambers text of Chapter 137 is the primary source. The principal objects of the Agency are:

1. To hold and manage, in Brunei and overseas, the **General Reserve Fund** and **all external assets of the Government**.
2. To provide the Government with money-management services on sums remitted to it and on the income and corporate actions that follow.
3. To recover and protect government or Agency property, including through subsidiaries and agents.

Section 18 of the same Act lists what it may hold: gold and other precious metals, real property, bank balances, treasury bills, and government-guaranteed securities, among other instruments the board approves. That is a reserve-and-investment statute, not a development-bank charter with a published industrial plan.

The Agency is a body corporate that can sue and be sued in its own name. It reports into the Ministry of Finance and Economy. Day-to-day names of officers are not something this page will take from an unsourced profile.

## How big is it?

There is no audited public figure. Brunei does not release an annual report or a holdings list. Industry trackers (SWF Institute, Global SWF and others) have circulated an estimate around **US$78 billion** in early 2026, up from figures in the low-US$70 billions in 2023. The US State Department has previously used a 2023 figure near US$73 billion. None of those is a BIA disclosure.

Read the estimate against population, not against ADIA. Brunei’s resident population is under half a million. A mid-sized book on a global league table is a very large book per citizen. That is the only scale comment that does not require a fake NAV.

## Where the money comes from

Oil and gas still dominate exports and the budget. The logic is the commodity-SWF logic: turn a depleting reserve into a financial endowment that can sit after the wells thin. BIA is the legal box that holds the savings sleeve. It is not, on the public record, a second MAS with a published FX-intervention book.

## What it is known to own

Because there is no holdings file, the portfolio is visible only when a transaction leaks.

**GP stake, August 2025.** Asia Asset Management, reporting Bloomberg and a person familiar with the matter, said BIA acquired a **nearly 20%** equity stake in the parent of Bridgewater Associates, funded in part by redeeming a Bridgewater strategy, and that BIA remained an investor in Bridgewater funds. Other institutions (Texas Teachers, OMERS, GIC, the IMF) have bought Bridgewater equity over the past decade. This page treats the 20% figure as a *reported* transaction, not as a filing we have seen.

**Real estate.** Brunei-linked entities have, over decades, been associated with landmark hotels and prime property in London and other cities. Treat historical press as historical press. Do not turn a 1990s hotel story into a 2026 position list.

**Domestic / strategic.** Occasional local technology and data projects are attributed to the state. Unless BIA is named in a primary notice, do not put them on this balance sheet.

## Governance and the Santiago test

The Santiago Principles ask for a written mandate, operational independence, and disclosure. Chapter 137 gives a written mandate. Independence and disclosure are another matter. Brunei is not a working IFSWF transparency case. There is no public performance letter of the GIC or NBIM type. For a counterparty that means: you can do a deal; you cannot underwrite the fund from a PDF.

The Sultan is head of state, prime minister and finance minister. That concentration is the system, not a footnote.

## How a universal owner should read it

BIA is a reminder that **size and disclosure are independent**. A mid-sized commodity fund can be a real LP, a real-estate buyer, and a GP-stake buyer while publishing nothing. League tables that add “US$78 billion” into a global total are adding a guess. If you are sizing the SWF universe for an IC memo, show BIA as a range.
