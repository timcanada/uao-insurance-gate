# CDPQ (La Caisse), explained

Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec now trades as **La Caisse**. It is a public-pension and insurance asset manager for **48** Québec depositors, not a sovereign wealth fund. The 2025 Annual Report printed net assets of **CAD $517.3 billion at 31 December 2025**. The weighted-average return on depositor funds was **9.3%** for the year, below the **10.9%** benchmark portfolio. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

La Caisse is **not** a sovereign wealth fund. It invests the savings of Québec pension and insurance plans. The legal person is still Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec; **La Caisse** is the registered brand. Charles Emond is President and Chief Executive Officer on the 2025 report.

The dual mandate, in the house’s own words: generate optimal long-term returns for the 48 depositors, who represent **over 6 million Quebecers**, and contribute to Québec’s economic development. The $100.1 billion Québec book is a **presence** figure, not a second AUM.

Compare it with [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) and [OTPP](/ontario-teachers-pension-plan-explained/), not with [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/). The Q&A “is CDPQ a SWF?” is the classification page; this URL is the institution page.

Credit ratings restated on the February release: **AAA** (DBRS, S&P, Fitch) and **Aaa** (Moody’s), stable outlook. That is a credit opinion, not an AUM.

## How a peer should read it

- **C$473 billion is dead.** Anyone still quoting the 2024 annual report is a year late.
- **9.3% lost to a 10.9% benchmark.** A good absolute year is not value-add. Say both numbers.
- **48 depositors, not one plan.** The headline return is a weighted average. The QPP sleeve is the largest exhibit, not the whole house.
- **La Caisse is the brand. CDPQ is the legal name.** Keep `/cdpq-explained/`. Do not farm a second slug.
