# What is a golden share?

A golden share is a reserved class of rights that can outvote, or sit outside, ordinary majority control. Companies Act 2006 does not define the phrase. The official explanatory notes on the Takeover Directive opt-in say the UK Government holds “golden shares” in formerly publicly-owned businesses, and that Article 11 does not apply where a Minister holds special rights. The European Court of Justice’s 13 May 2003 judgment in *Commission v United Kingdom* (C-98/01) held that the United Kingdom’s special share in BAA plc — a 15 percent voting cap plus consent rights over asset disposal, subsidiaries, and winding-up — restricted the free movement of capital under Article 56 EC. It is not a national-security notification statute. This page is not a ranking and not legal advice.

## A label in the notes, rights in a judgment

The official [Companies Act 2006 explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/notes/division/10/28/10/7/1) on the Takeover Directive opt-in (notes on section 966) are the sourced UK label:

1. The notes say the UK Government holds “golden shares” in a number of formerly publicly-owned businesses. The phrase is a **drafting and public-law label**, not a defined term in the operative sections.
2. Article 11 of the Directive (the breakthrough / opt-in rule) **does not apply** where a Minister of the Crown holds special rights in a company.
3. The European Court of Justice judgment of **13 May 2003** in Case **C-98/01** (*Commission of the European Communities v United Kingdom*) records the United Kingdom’s special share in **BAA plc**: no person could have voting rights attached to more than **15 percent** of the issued share capital, and specified actions — disposal of a designated airport, creating a subsidiary that was not wholly owned, or winding-up — needed the special shareholder’s written consent. The Court held those restrictions incompatible with **Article 56 EC**. Source: [EUR-Lex, Case C-98/01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:62001CJ0098).

A CIO comparing a UK target to a Washington filing should keep the instruments apart. A golden share is a **share-class and constitutional veto**. The [UK National Security and Investment Act](/what-is-a-national-security-screen-on-a-deal/) is a **statutory screen** that can make an unapproved completion void. [CFIUS](/foreign-investment-regulations-cfius/) is the US interagency review. [How SWFs think about sanctions](/how-do-swfs-think-about-sanctions/) is Santiago GAPP 19 and FSIA commercial-activity language, not a reserved share.
