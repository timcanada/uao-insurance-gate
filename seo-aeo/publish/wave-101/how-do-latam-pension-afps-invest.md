# How do Latin American AFPs invest?

Latin American AFPs invest inside a statute, not a stacked regional AUM. Chile’s Superintendencia still runs five multifondos (A–E) with official equity bands — Fund A may hold 40 to 80 percent in equities; Fund E may hold 0 to 5 percent — and Law 21.735 replaces that scheme with generational funds, with transition due in April 2027. Mexico splits the same job in two: the Afore administers the account; the Siefore holds the book. There is no official combined Latin American pension AUM. This page is not a ranking and not advice.

## The regime, not the pile

Chile’s Superintendencia de Pensiones [fondos de pensiones](https://www.spensiones.gob.cl/portal/institucional/594/w3-propertyvalue-9909.html) page is the sourced multifondo file (scheme in force since **26 September 2002**):

1. An affiliate’s compulsory contributions sit in an AFP. The person chooses one or two of five funds — **A** (más riesgoso) through **E** (más conservador) — by lifecycle and risk. Official **variable-income** bands: A **40–80 percent**; B **25–60 percent**; C **15–40 percent**; D **5–20 percent**; E **0–5 percent**.
2. The pension funds **belong to the affiliates**. They have no relation to the AFP’s own capital. Gains go to the individual capitalisation accounts.
3. Law **21.735** (Reforma Previsional) replaces multifondos with **Fondos Generacionales**. The Superintendencia’s [1 September 2026 consultation](https://www.spensiones.cl/portal/institucional/594/w3-article-17076.html) states the investment régime must be published by **1 September 2026** and the transition from multifondos is due in **April 2027**. Until then the A–E scheme stays in force.

Mexico is a different legal person. The [Ley del SAR](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LSAR.pdf) makes the **Afore** the administrator and the **Siefore** the investment company (Articles 18 and 39). That define already sits on [what an Afore is](/what-is-an-afore/).

The Wave 33 keeper already refused a stacked regional AUM. Brazil’s occupational complementary funds are not Chilean AFPs. Chile’s ESSF is a **stabilisation SWF**, not a pension. Do not file it here to fill a row.

## What an owner is actually buying

A manager selling into a Chilean AFP is selling into a **band the Superintendencia already wrote** — not into a homemade “LatAm pensions $2.7 trillion” slide.

A manager selling into Mexico is selling into a **Siefore** the Afore operates. Named-house keepers stay unminted until this desk fetches an official print. Do not invent Afore XXI Banorte, Previ, or Funpresp dollars.
