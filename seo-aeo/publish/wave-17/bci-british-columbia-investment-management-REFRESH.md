# BCI, explained

British Columbia Investment Management Corporation (BCI) is a Canadian Crown corporation that invests for British Columbia public-sector pension and institutional clients. It reported **$313.7 billion of gross assets** and **$265.4 billion of net assets at 31 March 2026**. The gap is the **Funding Program ($30.3 billion)** plus **other leverage ($18 billion)**. The live UAO page’s **$224.2 billion** line is a dead print. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/bci-british-columbia-investment-management/`

The live page this overwrites (20 June 2026) stamps **$224.2 billion** as if it were current AUM. Official About BCI leads with **$313.7 billion in gross assets as of 31 March 2026**. BCI’s own sentence is “one of Canada’s largest institutional investors.” [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) is larger (**C$793.3 billion** at 31 March 2026). Gross minus the two leverage lines is the net print: 313.7 − 30.3 − 18.0 = **265.4**. Sleeve weights on the investing-overview table are **percent of net AUM** and therefore sum to **118.4%** at the gross line. Do not call $313.7 billion “net.” Do not call $224.2 billion current.

## What is not official — do not put it back

## How a peer should read it

BCI is the British Columbia public-sector pool — Victoria headquarters, 33 clients, a large in-house book, and a **gross/net** print you have to keep separate. It is **not** [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/), **not** [PSP Investments](/psp-investments-canada-explained/), and **not** [AIMCo](/aimco-alberta-investment-management-corporation/). Anyone still writing **$224.2 billion** is a year-plus late.
