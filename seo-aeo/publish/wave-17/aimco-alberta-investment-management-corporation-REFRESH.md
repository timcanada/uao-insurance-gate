# AIMCo, explained

Alberta Investment Management Corporation (AIMCo) is a Canadian Crown corporation that invests for pension, endowment, insurance and government clients in Alberta. It reported **C$194.7 billion** of client assets under management at **31 December 2025**. The Balanced Fund returned **7.6%** (**C$13.1 billion**) and the Total Fund returned **7.5%**; both sat **2.7 percentage points below** their benchmarks. The live UAO page’s **CAD $160 billion** line and **“Canada’s largest”** claim are dead. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/aimco-alberta-investment-management-corporation/`

The live page this overwrites (20 June 2026) calls AIMCo “Canada’s largest pension fund manager,” stamps **CAD $160 billion** “as of 2024,” and invents a **650,000+** beneficiary count. Official is **C$194.7 billion** at **31 December 2025**, up from **C$179.6 billion** at year-end 2024. AIMCo’s own sentence is “one of Canada’s largest.” [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) is larger (**C$793.3 billion** at 31 March 2026). `/aimco-explained/` **404s**. Do not mint it. Keep this URL.

AIMCo’s Balanced Fund is a **typical client mix** across asset classes. The Total Fund is the **aggregate of all client accounts**. Each client sets its own long-term asset mix. Do not treat the Balanced Fund as every client’s return.

The release blames the 2.7-point shortfall on “a challenging year for private markets and the use of public market-linked benchmarks within private asset classes.”

## What is not official — do not put it back

- **CAD $160 billion.** Dead size. Official is **C$194.7 billion**.
- **“Canada’s largest.”** CPP is larger. AIMCo’s own sentence is “one of Canada’s largest.”
- A homemade **650,000+** beneficiary count. The 2025 results release does not print one.
- Dollarizing C$194.7 billion into a US-dollar AUM.
- Treating the Balanced Fund as the Total Fund, or the other way around.
- Minting `/aimco-explained/`.

## How a peer should read it

AIMCo is part of the Canadian pension-model cluster — in-house capability, private markets, a provincial client book — but it is **not** [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/), **not** [PSP Investments](/psp-investments-canada-explained/), and **not** [BCI](/bci-british-columbia-investment-management/). The sponsor set is Alberta public-sector and related clients. Public equities carried 2025. Private markets did not. Both headline funds **lost** to their benchmarks by the same 2.7 points.
