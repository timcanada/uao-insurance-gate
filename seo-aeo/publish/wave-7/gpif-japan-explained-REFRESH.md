# GPIF, explained

Japan’s Government Pension Investment Fund is the world’s largest dedicated public pension. Official investment assets were **¥293,643.7 billion at 31 March 2026**. The FY2025 return (1 April 2025–31 March 2026) was **16.47%**, or **¥41,399.5 billion**. The policy mix is four liquid sleeves near 25% each. Alternatives are capped at 5% and were **1.70%** of the book at end-December 2025. This is **not** a sovereign wealth fund. Not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research · **Canonical URL:** `/gpif-japan-explained/`

The live page this overwrites said FY2025 returned **15.83%**. That number is wrong. GPIF’s own English annual-report summary, dated 3 July 2026, prints **16.47%**.

Do not convert those yen figures to a fake-precise US dollar headline. The yen/dollar rate is why dollar studies can put [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) ahead of GPIF as the largest *asset owner* without GPIF’s yen book shrinking.

Among **pensions**, GPIF remains number one on a beneficiary test. Among **all asset owners**, Norway’s GPFG is the largest book that publishes a current official value (22,683 billion kroner at 30 June 2026). See [largest pensions](/largest-pension-funds-in-the-world/) and [largest asset owners](/largest-asset-owners-in-the-world/).

## How it invests

The policy portfolio is deliberately plain:

- Domestic equities ~25%
- Foreign equities ~25%
- Domestic bonds ~25%
- Foreign bonds ~25%

Deviation bands sit around those weights. Most of the book is indexed. At this size, active trading *is* the market. Cost and capacity are why the model stays simple.

Alternatives (infrastructure, private equity, real estate) exist inside a **5%** cap. At end-December 2025 they were **1.70%**. That is not the Canadian model. It is a liquidity-first reserve for an ageing public pension system.

## The 2014 equity pivot

The consequential modern decision was the 2014 policy-portfolio overhaul that raised the equity sleeves toward the four-way mix used today. Because the book is national in scale, a few points of reallocation are a global flow. Any hint of a future shift still moves JGB and equity desks. That is a fact about size, not a reason to call GPIF a hedge fund.

## How a peer should read it

- **Universal owner by construction.** Four market sleeves at this size *are* the market.
- **Fee compression.** Mandates here pay indexing economics. Do not pitch a 2-and-20 story to this IC.
- **Not a SWF.** Rankings that put GPIF in a sovereign-wealth top three have the wrong perimeter.
- **FX is not performance.** A weak yen can make GPIF look smaller than GPFG in dollars in the same week the yen book made money.
