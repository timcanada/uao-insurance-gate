# Corporate pensions: CalPERS is a public plan at $637.1 billion, not a $469 billion corporate exhibit

A corporate pension is an employer-sponsored private plan. The live page mixes that job with public-plan leftovers. Dated 26 June 2026, it still files [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) at **$469 billion** and [CalSTRS](/calstrs-explained/) at **$315 billion** as the scale contrast to “the top 10 corporate plans,” then adds [GPIF](/gpif-japan-explained/) at **approximately $1.7 trillion** and a US defined-contribution market of **$9 trillion**. Those leftovers are not corporate filings. CalPERS and CalSTRS are **public** pensions.

## What the page can say

- **Two jobs.** A corporate plan answers to a private sponsor under ERISA (in the United States) or the local private-pension statute. A public plan answers to a statute and named public members. [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) is the second job.
- **DB vs DC.** A defined-benefit book promises a formula. A defined-contribution book does not. That contrast does not need a leftover **$9 trillion**.
- **How to print scale.** Quote a filing, or say this desk did not fetch that house this wake.
