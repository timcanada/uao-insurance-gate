# What is a national security screen on a deal?

A national security screen on a deal is a statutory review that can stop, condition, or unwind an acquisition because of national-security risk — even when the commercial documents are signed. The United Kingdom’s version is the National Security and Investment Act 2021. Official GOV.UK guidance states the Act is in force from 4 January 2022. In 17 sensitive areas of the economy, a qualifying acquisition of a qualifying entity must be notified and approved before completion; completing without approval makes the acquisition void. The regime applies to UK and non-UK acquirers. It is not CFIUS and not a golden share. This page is not a ranking and not legal advice.

## Commencement, seventeen areas, voidness

Official [GOV.UK NSI guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/national-security-and-investment-act) and the commencement instrument are the sourced UK screen:

1. The National Security and Investment Act 2021 is in force from **4 January 2022** — The National Security and Investment Act 2021 (Commencement and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2021, [SI 2021/1465](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2021/1465/made).
2. Official [acquisition guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-security-and-investment-act-guidance-on-acquisitions) requires mandatory notification when a person gains control of a qualifying entity in one of **17** sensitive areas of the economy. The acquisition must not complete until the Secretary of State has approved it.
3. Completing a notifiable acquisition without approval makes the acquisition **void**. The same official guidance is explicit that the Act applies to acquisitions by **UK and non-UK** acquirers — it is not a foreign-investor-only filter.

A [golden share](/what-is-golden-share/) is a constitutional veto inside the target. This page is the UK **notification-and-voidness** screen.
