# China Investment Corporation (CIC), explained

China Investment Corporation is the People’s Republic of China’s sovereign wealth fund, founded **29 September 2007** with registered capital of **US$200 billion**. Its 9 December 2025 release of the 2024 annual report put **total assets at US$1.57 trillion** and **net assets at US$1.37 trillion** at 31 December 2024. The 10-year annualized net return was **6.92% in USD**. Overseas investing sits in CIC International and CIC Capital; Central Huijin holds domestic state financial-institution equity. This is not investment advice.

Do **not** treat US$1.57 trillion as “overseas AUM.” CIC’s own total includes Central Huijin’s domestic financial-institution equity. That is why a tracker that prints “CIC ~$1.3 trillion” and a filing that prints US$1.57 trillion can both be describing different perimeters of the same house.

CIC has not published a later annual report as of this draft. Year-end 2024 is the current official vintage. Do not invent a mid-2026 CIC NAV.

## What CIC says it is

CIC’s own sentence: it was established as a vehicle to **diversify China’s foreign-exchange holdings** and to **seek maximum returns for its shareholder within acceptable risk tolerance**. It is incorporated under China’s Company Law. The State Council exercises the shareholder’s rights. The three governing bodies are the Board of Directors, the Board of Supervisors, and the Executive Committee.

Chairman **Zhang Qingsong** signed the 2024 annual-report message published on 9 December 2025. Do not invent a President or CIO title from press.

CIC states that it implements the [Santiago Principles](/santiago-principles/). That is CIC’s claim, not a UAO audit.

## Three subsidiaries (official names only)

CIC’s “Who we are” page lists three, and only three:

1. **CIC International Co., Ltd. (CIC International)** — overseas. Public equity and bonds; hedge-fund and multi-asset; private equity and private credit; real estate, infrastructure, resources, commodities, agriculture; bilateral and multilateral funds.
2. **CIC Capital Corporation (CIC Capital)** — overseas. Direct and long-term assets. Incorporated 2015 on CIC’s own FAQ.
3. **Central Huijin Investment Ltd. (Central Huijin)** — domestic. Equity in key state-owned financial institutions in China. CIC says Huijin does not interfere in day-to-day operations of portfolio companies.

CIC’s own line: **strict firewalls** separate the overseas books (International + Capital) from Huijin’s domestic equity job.

Overseas offices CIC lists: CIC International (Hong Kong) Co., Ltd., New York representative office, London representative office.

Do not reprint the live page’s “China Investment Corporation International Holdings Limited” or “China Securities Investment Funds Management Company Limited.” Those names are not on CIC’s English site.

## How a peer should read the number

- **Total vs net.** Use both, labelled. Total is the larger print. Net is the closer read of equity.
- **Huijin is not the SWF sleeve.** A GP pitching “CIC” needs to know which legal person is across the table.
- **Vintage.** This is a 2024 year-end annual report released in December 2025. [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/) already publishes a 30 June 2026 kroner figure. Dollar league tables that freeze CIC at YE2024 and refresh Norway weekly will move the order without either house trading.
- **SAFE is a different institution.** Do not invent a SAFE AUM and stack it next to CIC as “China’s two $1.4 trillion funds.”

Among [sovereign wealth funds](/sovereign-wealth-fund/), CIC is one of the few giants that *does* publish a balance-sheet total. Among [largest asset owners](/largest-asset-owners-in-the-world/), it sits in the same conversation as GPFG and [GPIF](/gpif-japan-explained/) — different currencies, different jobs, different as-of dates.
