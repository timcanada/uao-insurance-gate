# How search firms run a CIO mandate

A search firm runs a CIO mandate as a written, exclusive consulting assignment — not a résumé blast. The Association of Executive Search and Leadership Consultants already requires candor, a written engagement, conflict disclosure, and work from the first brief through onboarding. Universal Asset Owners maps that category on the [executive-search directory](/directory/executive-search/). This page is not a placement guarantee and not compensation advice.

## What a professional search already is

AESC’s [Client Bill of Rights](https://www.aesc.org/standards/aesc-client-bill-of-rights/) (page dated **15 October 2025**) is the public standard. Three facts from that text:

1. AESC member firms **operate on a retained and exclusive basis** for executive search and give advice from the beginning to the conclusion of each assignment.
2. The terms of the engagement **should be in writing**: the assignment, the lead advisor, the scope, the timing, the fees and payment schedule, deliverables, and specific terms that might include **guarantees, off-limits, conflicts, and data management**.
3. Members **avoid conflicts** with clients, candidates, and outside parties — and where a potential conflict exists, they **disclose and resolve it mutually**, including a waiver when appropriate. Confidential information is never used for the consultant’s own gain. Responsibility **does not end when the candidate accepts**; members remain available for onboarding.

That is the CIO job. A public pension will also ask who was on the long list and why the winner can sit in front of a legislature. A sovereign search is often quieter. A family-office search is a confidentiality problem first — already defined on [how family offices hire CIOs](/how-do-family-offices-hire-cios/).

What “retained” means as a contract form is on [what a retained search is](/what-is-a-retained-search/).

## Questions the board should put in the minute

1. Who is the working partner, and will they sit in the interviews?
2. How do you search people who are not looking — without making the role a rumour?
3. Where is the conflict when you also place people at the managers we hire?
4. What will you refuse to put in the candidate pack?
