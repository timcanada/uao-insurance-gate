# How family offices hire CIOs

A family office hires a CIO through a principal, not through a public RFP. The buying centre is the family — sometimes one person, sometimes a next-generation committee that only looks like a board. Confidentiality outranks the long list. This explainer sits beside Universal Asset Owners' [family-office definition](/what-is-a-family-office/) and [first-year briefing](/careers/intelligence/first-year-inside-a-family-office/). It is not tax, estate, or compensation advice and not a recommendation of any search firm.

**Do not treat this page as a second family-office definition.** Do not mint `/family-office/` or `/family-office-cio-hire/`. Do not invent a family-office AUM or a pay range.

## The principal is the panel

A [family office](/what-is-a-family-office/) is a private organisation that manages the investments, wealth and affairs of a single ultra-wealthy family. The live define page is the job: single ownership and alignment. A public pension [succession](/careers/intelligence/succession-inside-a-public-plan/) will be asked who was on the long list. A family office will be asked who still speaks to the principal after the hire.

Universal Asset Owners already publishes that it reaches **85%** of the world’s largest family offices on [Readership](/readership/). That is a circulation fact, not a licence to invent a sector AUM or a CIO salary on this page.

[Executive search](/directory/executive-search/) firms already work this room. Listing a firm here is not a recommendation. Three sourced facts or the firm does not get a child page.

## What the hire actually tests

1. **Whose voice is the mandate.** Principal, next generation, or a committee that has not written the rule.
2. **What you may say outside the house.** There is no “market colour” that is safe by default.
3. **The other functions.** Tax, estate, philanthropy and administration sit in the same file as the portfolio.
4. **Internal versus rented.** Are you hiring a CIO office, or a person who will still call an [OCIO](/what-is-an-ocio/)?
5. **Exit.** How does the last CIO leave, and who still has the passwords?
