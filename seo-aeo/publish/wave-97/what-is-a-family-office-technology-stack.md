# What is a family-office technology stack?

A family-office technology stack is the set of systems that keep one book: the investment ledger, the confidentiality of the family file, and a number that matches the custodian. Universal Asset Owners already defines the office as the owner of that book. The OCC already defines bank custody as settlement, safekeeping, and reporting. This page is not a vendor catalogue and not a product endorsement.

**Do not treat this page as a second family-office definition or a second technology-directory overwrite.** Those keepers are `/what-is-a-family-office/` and `/directory/technology/`. Do not mint `/family-office/`, `/family-office-tech-stack/`, or `/fo-tech-stack-explained/`. Do not invent vendor logos or a stack SKU.

## What the office is already buying

[What is a family office?](/what-is-a-family-office/) already says the point of the structure is **one book, one set of incentives** — investing plus tax, estate, consolidated reporting, and administration. The stack is how that book is kept. Three sourced facts, not a product list:

1. The family office is the **owner**, not a private bank and not an OCIO unless the family has rented the CIO seat.
2. Bank custody, as the OCC already prints it, is **settlement, safekeeping, and reporting** of customers’ marketable securities and cash — the number the board pack and the auditor can use. How an owner chooses that bank sits on [how owners pick a custodian](/how-do-owners-pick-a-custodian/).
3. The [technology directory](/directory/technology/) already names the failure mode: who owns the data; who can see the family file; how an investment committee extracts a number that matches the custodian.

That is the stack. Portfolio software, a data room, identity, and cyber sit under those three jobs. A founder Slack is not a ledger. A bank portal that cannot see private-markets NAV is not one book.

The first year inside the office — confidentiality first, then the book — already sits on [first year inside a family office](/careers/intelligence/first-year-inside-a-family-office/).

## What a principal should ask before a demo

1. Who owns the data on exit, and in what file?
2. Can the investment committee see public and private holdings in one look-through that matches the custodian?
3. Who can open the family or beneficiary file, and is that logged?
4. What will you refuse to put in a security review?

Do not flatten this page onto [how owners pick a custodian](/how-do-owners-pick-a-custodian/). Custody is the bank. The stack is everything that has to reconcile to that bank.
