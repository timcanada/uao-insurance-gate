# Cybersecurity for sovereign funds, pensions and family offices

Asset owners buy cybersecurity for a failure mode that is not a consumer breach blog: a leak of a beneficiary file, a family office, a sanctions list, or a board pack. This hub is Universal Asset Owners' map of that category. It is not a ranking and not a security assessment.

The Wave 1 [technology hub](/directory/technology/) already covers the stack. **This page is the cybersecurity job.** A listing here is not an endorsement. Featured placements are marked paid.

Last updated: 26 August 2026.

## What owners are actually buying

A public pension buys identity, vendor risk, and a process that will survive a records request after an incident.

A sovereign or strategic fund buys a screen that understands sanctions, state-adjacent counterparties, and the difference between a savings mandate and a development mandate.

A family office buys silence. A leak is estate risk. The “enterprise platform that won the last bank RFP” is sometimes the wrong product for a twelve-person office.

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) are not hedge funds. The buyer is a fiduciary desk, a family, or a ministry — not a trading floor. [Ethics](/ethics/) still governs anything that looks like coverage.

If you sell into this room, say which of those jobs you have closed. If you buy, ask who can see the file and what happens when you leave.

## The questions a CIO, CISO or principal should ask

1. Who is the working partner, and have they sat through an owner incident — not only a manager incident?
2. Where does the beneficiary or family file live, and who can export it?
3. How do you handle a vendor that also serves the managers we hire?
4. What will you refuse to put in the board pack after a breach?
