# HOOPP, explained

Healthcare of Ontario Pension Plan is the Ontario healthcare workers’ pension, not leftover Ontario Teachers’, leftover OMERS, leftover CAAT, and not leftover OPTrust. The official [2025 Annual Report](https://hoopp.com/docs/default-source/investments-library/annual-reports/hoopp-2025-annual-report.pdf) prints net assets available for benefits of **C$131,936 million** at **31 December 2025**. Investment assets were **C$272,195 million**. Investment liabilities were **C$140,692 million**. Those three lines are not interchangeable. Pension obligations were **C$120,833 million**. Surplus was **C$11,103 million**. House net return was **7.7 percent**. Ten-year net return was **7.8 percent**. Funded status was **109 percent** on both the smoothed and net-asset-value cards. Print Canadian dollars. Do not dollarize. This page is not a ranking and not advice.

**Do not treat this page as a leftover Ontario Teachers’ overwrite, a leftover OMERS overwrite, a leftover CAAT overwrite, or a leftover OPTrust overwrite.** Do not mint `/hoopp-explained/` or `/healthcare-of-ontario/`. Do not treat the house **C$131.9 billion** card as a second AUM. That card is the **C$131,936 million** statement line rounded. Do not treat a rounded press **C$132 billion** line as the statement.

## What is official (31 December 2025)

The consolidated statement of financial position is the sourced line. The report prints Canadian **$ millions**:

1. **Net assets available for benefits** **C$131,936 million** at **31 December 2025** (2024 **C$123,017 million**). Investment assets **C$272,195 million** is not net assets. Investment liabilities **C$140,692 million** sit on the other side of that book. Other assets and contribution receivables sit above the net-assets line. Those prints are not interchangeable. Do not treat leftover OTPP, leftover OMERS **$145.2 billion**, leftover CAAT, leftover OPTrust, leftover MassPRIM **$73 billion**, or leftover Ohio PERS **$46 billion** as this filing. Do not dollarize.
2. **Pension-obligation cards on the same statement.** Pension obligations **C$120,833 million**. Surplus **C$11,103 million**. Those two lines are not interchangeable with net assets available for benefits.
3. **Returns and funded cards.** House net return **7.7 percent** (2024 **9.7 percent**), printed net of all investment costs. Ten-year net return **7.8 percent**. Twenty-year net return **8.4 percent**. Net investment income **C$9,720 million**. Investment operating expenses **C$382 million**. Funded ratio **109 percent** on both the smoothed-asset and net-asset-value cards (2024 smoothed **111 percent**; 2024 net-asset-value **109 percent**). Those official prints are not interchangeable.

Do not flatten HOOPP into [Ontario Teachers’](/ontario-teachers-pension-plan-explained/), [OMERS](/omers-explained/), [CAAT](/caat-pension/), [OPTrust](/optrust/), or [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/). OTPP is the teacher plan. OMERS is the municipal plan. CAAT is the colleges plan. OPTrust is the OPSEU plan. CPP is the Canada Pension Plan investment board. This is **one Ontario healthcare-pension file**.

## What an owner is actually buying

Counsel selling “HOOPP AUM” has to name the book and the currency. The **C$131,936 million** line is net assets available for benefits. The **C$272,195 million** line is investment assets. The **C$140,692 million** line is investment liabilities. The **C$11,103 million** line is surplus. Those are not the same mandate.
