# What is a future generations fund?

A future generations fund is a **savings book whose legal job is to hold resource or fiscal surplus for people who are not yet the spending generation** — not a synonym for every long-horizon sovereign or pension. Named exhibits that print official figures: [NSIA](/nsia-nigeria-sovereign-investment-authority-explained/)’s **Future Generations Fund** sleeve (part of a **$3.40 billion** NAV at 31 December 2025); the [Alaska Permanent Fund](/alaska-permanent-fund-explained/) (**$91.35 billion at 30 June 2026**); [NZ Super](/new-zealand-superannuation-fund-explained/) (**NZ$85.05 billion** audited at 30 June 2025). [CPP Investments](/cpp-investments-explained/) is a **public pension**, not a future generations fund. This is not investment advice.

## What the label means

Three tests. Miss one and you are stretching the words.

1. **A savings rule, not a pension payroll.** Contributions come from resource surplus, a fiscal rule, or a one-time transfer. Benefits are not a member’s accrued pension.
2. **A spending constraint that protects corpus.** POMV, a fiscal rule, or a “do not spend the principal” statute. Without that, it is just a pot the finance ministry can raid.
3. **Named future beneficiaries in the statute or mandate** — a later generation of residents, or a later decade of a universal pension the state has not yet started drawing.

That is narrower than “[sovereign wealth fund](/sovereign-wealth-fund/)” and much narrower than “[universal owner](/universal-owner/).” A SWF can be a stabilizer, a development fund, or a reserve manager. A public pension can invest for **current and future contributors** without being a future-generations *savings* vehicle.

Do not use the Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute as a source on this URL.

**NSIA** is the house that *names* a Future Generations Fund. The 2025 USD statements: three ring-fenced books — Stabilisation, Future Generations, Nigeria Infrastructure. FGF reportable assets **$1,557,153 thousand**. That is a sleeve, not the Group NAV. Do not call $1.56 billion “NSIA AUM.”

**Alaska** is the clean US exhibit. Two accounts, a POMV draw, a Permanent Fund Dividend that is a *legislative* transfer from earnings — not a second AUM. The 2025 PFD was **$1,000**. Do not invent a 2026 PFD. Live **$83 billion** is dead.

**NZ Super** is a pre-fund created under the 2001 Act; it began investing in **September 2003**. The live page’s “established 2007” and **NZD 71 billion** lines are both wrong. First modelled drawdowns have been discussed for **later this decade**, not “from 2031” as a frozen fact. It is **not** a KiwiSaver product.

**GPFG** has “pension” in the name and is a [sovereign wealth fund](/sovereign-wealth-fund/). Use **kroner**. The end-2023 **$1.4 trillion** line is a year-and-a-half and a currency conversion behind.

**CPP is the classification error.** More than **22 million** contributors and beneficiaries; arm’s length from governments; [IFSWF excludes member-owned public pensions](https://www.ifswf.org/what-is-a-sovereign-wealth-fund). A 25-year horizon does not turn it into a future-generations *fund*. Keep `/is-cpp-investments-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/` for the SWF question.

**Refuse:** Abu Dhabi SGRF AUM. ADIA AUM. A “Denmark intergenerational equity fund” that has no official vehicle on this page. Oman’s **Future Fund Oman** is a **domestic project book** (OMR 2 billion capital) — see [OIA](/oman-investment-authority-explained/), and do not file it here as a savings FGF.

Ireland’s **Future Ireland Funds** sit beside [ISIF](/isif-ireland-strategic-investment-fund-explained/) at the NTMA. They are a different legal book. Do not add them to ISIF, and do not invent a 2025 headline for them here.

## How a peer should read it

- **Name the statute, then the size.** “Long-term” is not a mandate.
- **NZ$71bn / CAD $580bn / $1.4T / $83bn are dead.** Use the official as-ofs above.
- **CPP is a pension.** If the slide needs a Canadian savings SWF, it does not have one on this page.
