# How asset owners pick a custodian

An owner picks a custodian for a number the board, the auditor, and the beneficiary file can all use: safekeeping, settlement, and reporting — not a logo on a pitch book. The U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency already defines bank custody that way. Universal Asset Owners maps the category on the [custody directory](/directory/custody-administration/). This page is not a ranking and not operational advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second custody-directory overwrite.** Do not mint `/pension-custodian/`, `/how-to-choose-a-custodian/`, or `/what-is-a-multi-custodian-model/`. Do not fill unsourced bank logos. Do not invent a house AUM for a custodian or an owner.

## What “custody” already means in a supervisor’s file

The OCC’s public custody-services page says custody services provided by a bank typically include the **settlement, safekeeping, and reporting** of customers’ marketable securities and cash. Securities lending, on that same page, is an optional income overlay on custody assets — a loan to approved borrowers on a short-term basis — not the reason you hired the bank.

That is the job. An owner who cannot reconcile private-markets NAV to the custodian cash account does not have a “technology problem.” They have a number the board cannot minute.

## What each owner type is actually buying

A public pension buys a book that can survive a records request and a legislative hearing. The failure mode is a NAV that does not match the board pack.

A sovereign or strategic fund buys cross-border safekeeping, sanctions screening, and a book that can be shown to a ministry.

A family office buys confidentiality and the ability to see public and private holdings in one place without putting the family file in a founder Slack.

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) are not asset managers. The custodian works for the owner. That is a [fiduciary](/fiduciary-duty-explained-institutional/) fact, not a sales line.

## Four questions that belong in the RFP

1. Who is the working client lead, and have they run an *owner* book — not only a manager book?
2. How do you reconcile private-markets NAV to the custodian cash account?
3. Where is the data stored, who can see the beneficiary or family file, and what happens on exit?
4. What will you refuse to put in an SLA?

If you need two books on purpose, that is a multi-custodian design. That keeper is [what a multi-custodian model is](/what-is-a-multi-custodian-model/). Do not invent it here.
