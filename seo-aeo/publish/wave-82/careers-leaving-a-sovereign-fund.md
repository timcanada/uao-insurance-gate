# Leaving a sovereign wealth fund — what the next seat actually is

Leaving a sovereign wealth fund is not the reverse of a banking tour. You are leaving an owner seat — a mandate, a public file, and a horizon that does not reset when you do. This Careers Intelligence briefing sits beside Universal Asset Owners' [playbook for moving into a sovereign fund](/careers/intelligence/moving-into-a-sovereign-wealth-fund/). It is not outplacement, immigration, or compensation advice.

**Do not treat this page as a second moving-into playbook.** Do not invent a sovereign-fund AUM to decorate an exit story.

## The exit is part of the mandate

[Asset owners](/what-is-an-asset-owner/) hold capital for their own account. A sovereign wealth fund is one of those owners. The [IFSWF](https://www.ifswf.org/) exists because those houses sit in a public, state-linked file — Santiago-era membership, not a product shelf.

The live moving-into playbook already says what fails on the way in: people “come for a few years but don't always stay.” The same sentence is the exit risk. If the house cannot name where alumni go, the next employer will ask why. If you cannot say what you were allowed to decide, you will be hired as a tourist.

Permanent capital and a long horizon are why the job felt slow. They are also why the next seat often feels loud. A GP, a bank, or a search firm is buying access and judgement — not a licence to reprint the fund’s book.

## What to pressure-test before you resign

1. **What you can say.** The file is public in places and confidential in others. Ask counsel what travels.
2. **Decision rights you actually held.** Committee work is not the same as a personal book.
3. **Where alumni sit.** Three named seats beat a rumour about “London.”
4. **Compensation shape.** Do not anchor on a blog range. Ask for the policy on both sides.
5. **Conflicts.** A raise, a co-invest, or a secondaries book that touched your old desk is a process, not a reunion.

## For employers and search firms

Owners already use [Careers](/careers/) when they are hiring. If you are recruiting someone out of a sovereign fund, say what seat you are actually offering — GP, LP, OCIO, family office, or ministry — and do not treat the Talent Desk as a surprise.
