# NSIA (Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority), explained

The Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority is the Federation’s sovereign wealth manager under the **NSIA Act 2011**. It is **three ring-fenced books** — Stabilisation, Future Generations, and Nigeria Infrastructure — not a single **$1.6 billion** pile. The 2 April 2026 results printed Group net asset value of **$3,397.3 million** and total assets of **$3,418.7 million at 31 December 2025**. Managing Director and CEO: **Aminu Umar-Sadiq**. This is not investment advice.

## The three books (official assets, not invented weights)

The 2025 special-purpose USD statements name three ring-fenced funds and print **reportable fund assets** at 31 December 2025 ($ thousand):

| Book | Mandate (NSIA’s words) | Reportable assets | Share of $3,418.7m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stabilisation Fund (SF)** | Stabilisation support to the Federation in times of economic stress | **$463.0 million** ($462,989 thousand) | ~14% |
| **Future Generations Fund (FGF)** | Diversified growth investments; a savings base for when hydrocarbon reserves are exhausted | **$1,557.2 million** ($1,557,153 thousand) | ~46% |
| **Nigeria Infrastructure Fund (NIF)** | Infrastructure projects in Nigeria that meet a return test and a development test | **$1,398.5 million** ($1,398,519 thousand) | ~41% |

Those percentages are **derived from NSIA’s own asset lines**. They are not a policy target. Do not put the live 30–40 / 35–45 / 15–25 split back. Do not treat a sleeve as Group NAV.

Segment **profit before tax** in the same note ($ thousand): SF **$5.2 million**, FGF **$81.1 million**, NIF **$20.7 million**, Group **$107.1 million**. That is a P&L split, not an allocation.

## What it is

A Federation investment authority for **excess hydrocarbon revenues**, not a member pension and not the Central Bank’s reserves. NSIA’s homepage: it manages Nigeria’s sovereign wealth fund; seed capital **US$1 billion** in 2013; IFSWF / [Santiago Principles](/santiago-principles/) signatory; PRI signatory (2025, on the same homepage timeline).

Board named on the 2025 USD statements: Chairman **Segun Ogunsanya**; Managing Director & CEO **Aminu Umar-Sadiq**; Executive Director / CIO **Kolawole Owodunni**.

The 2025 results also walk a few **programme** facts that are not AUM: PFI operatorship transferred to the Ministry of Finance Incorporated; MedServe oncology network (3 centres consolidated, 8 under construction, 11 expected by Q3 2026); a **$50 million** NSIA–JICA Impact Innovation Fund ($20 million NSIA / $14 million Japanese grant / $16 million expected from DFIs). Those are commitments and projects. Do not add them to $3.40 billion.

**Refuse:** an oil-price contribution threshold of “$70–75.” A 2012–2022 “5–6% blended return.” Homemade GIC, MGX, Angola, GEPF, or Egypt AUM on this URL. A population figure as if it were a financial metric.

## How a peer should read it

- **>$1.6 billion is dead.** Official NAV is **$3.40 billion** at 31 December 2025.
- **Three books.** FGF is the largest asset sleeve. Stabilisation is the liquid fiscal buffer. NIF is the domestic development book — see [strategic investment fund](/strategic-investment-fund/).
- **Core TCI is the score they want used.** 2025 Core TCI **$320.2 million**. FX swings are separated for a reason (naira **appreciated 6.5%** in 2025 after a **71%** devaluation in 2024, on NSIA’s own sentence).
- **Do not invent sleeve policy weights.** Quote the statement lines.
