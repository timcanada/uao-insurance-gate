# New York State Common Retirement Fund, explained

The New York State Common Retirement Fund holds and invests the assets of the New York State and Local Retirement System. Comptroller **Thomas P. DiNapoli**, sole trustee, reported an estimated **$295.4 billion at 31 March 2026** and an **11.94%** fiscal-year return. The Comptroller’s office later headlined **$309.7 billion** at the end of the first quarter of FY2026–27, with a **6.12%** quarterly return. The Fund serves nearly **1.3 million** members, retirees and beneficiaries. This is not investment advice.

## What it is

The Comptroller is the **sole trustee**. That is the New York model — not a CalPERS-style board of administration. OSC’s 2026 releases name DiNapoli; they do not hand you a separately branded “CIO of NYCRF” to invent.

The Fund is the investment book of **NYSLRS** (Employees’ Retirement System and Police and Fire Retirement System). It is a US public pension, not a sovereign wealth fund. Compare it with [CalPERS](/calpers-explained-largest-us-pension-fund/) and [CalSTRS](/calstrs-explained/), not with [NBIM](/norges-bank-investment-management-nbim/).

The 5.9% assumed return is the number OSC wants you to notice: it is conservative relative to many US publics. A year that prints 11.94% is a good year, not a new assumption.

## How a peer should read it

- **$226.8 billion is dead.** Anyone still quoting that figure is a year late.
- **Funded status lags the return.** 92.2% is 31 March 2025. The 11.94% year is not yet a 2026 funded ratio.
- **Sole trustee is the product.** If you cannot speak to the Comptroller’s office, you are not covering this fund.
- **Not NYCERS.** Different legal person. Do not mash the city systems into this book.
