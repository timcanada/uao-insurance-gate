# Temasek vs GIC: What Is the Difference?

Temasek is a commercial investment company that owns the assets on its own balance sheet. GIC is a professional fund manager of assets the Government of Singapore still owns. As at 31 March 2026 Temasek’s mark-to-market net portfolio value was S$518 billion (US$401 billion). GIC does not publish AUM; its rolling 20-year real return to the same date was 3.4% (5.6% USD nominal). Temasek can hold Singapore companies; GIC invests the long-horizon foreign-reserve sleeve. This is not investment advice.

**Last updated:** 26 August 2026 · **Desk:** UAO Research

The Ministry of Finance’s own reserves page, last updated 4 March 2026, is the cleanest official line: the Government’s assets are *mainly managed by GIC*; the Government is the *sole equity shareholder of Temasek*, and Temasek owns what sits on Temasek’s balance sheet. That one legal distinction drives mandate, geography, disclosure, and how a counterparty should underwrite a deal.

Do not add the two size numbers. One is audited NPV. The other is a hole GIC leaves on purpose.

## What Temasek is

Temasek Holdings was incorporated in 1974 to hold government-owned commercial assets. It still does that job, but the book is no longer a domestic holding company with a foreign sleeve. As at 31 March 2026 the portfolio sat in three segments that Temasek has kept near 40/40/20 since 2018:

- **Temasek Portfolio Companies (TPCs) — 43%.** Singapore-based operating companies, often concentrated listed stakes. Ten-year IRR 8.1%.
- **Global Direct Investments (GDIs) — 38%.** Higher-risk, higher-conviction book. Ten-year IRR 7.6%.
- **Partnerships, funds, and AMCs (PFAs) — 19%.** Alternatives and manager stakes. Ten-year IRR 7.7%.

Half the portfolio was liquid and listed; half was unlisted assets and funds. About 52% of portfolio companies are Singapore-headquartered; only 27% of *underlying* exposure is Singapore — the TPCs earn a large share of revenue abroad. FY2026 is the first year Temasek reports the whole unlisted book mark-to-market. On the old book-value method the NPV would have been S$486 billion; the MTM uplift on the remaining quarter was S$32 billion.

From 1 April 2026 Temasek split the operating companies into Temasek Singapore, Temasek Global Investments, and Temasek Partnership Solutions. That is an internal map, not a second mandate.

## What GIC is

GIC (GIC Private Limited) was set up in 1981 to manage the long-horizon tranche of official reserves, separate from MAS’s monetary-policy book. MOF’s sentence: GIC is “a professional fund manager that manages Government assets.” Its mandate is to preserve and enhance the **international purchasing power** of those reserves.

That is why the only number GIC treats as the score is the rolling 20-year real return. For 1 April 2006 to 31 March 2026 that return was **3.4%** after global inflation, on a **5.6%** USD nominal. CNA noted the 3.4% is the lowest 20-year real print in six years (the prior-year window, to 31 March 2025, was 3.8%). GIC says the drop is what you get when a rolling window drops an old strong year and adds a more cautious one, and when the house has been running a lower-risk mix.

From 1 April 2026 GIC is moving to a **Strategic Portfolio** of three groups — equities (growth), fixed income (income), real assets (inflation) — and the GIC Portfolio is supposed to beat that Strategic Portfolio inside a risk budget. In the year to 31 March 2026 GIC said the *share* of equities rose and fixed income fell; real assets were stable. It did **not** republish last year’s 51/26/23 split as a current point estimate. Geography, which it does publish: **Americas 53%, Asia-Pacific 22%, Europe/Middle East/Africa 19%, global 6%**. The US is the largest single market.

GIC invests that sleeve outside the domestic listed market for a policy reason MOF and GIC have both stated for years: a reserve manager buying Singapore assets at this scale would bid up local prices and blur monetary policy. Temasek is the vehicle that *can* own Singapore companies.

## Three pillars, not two

Singapore’s reserve system is MAS + GIC + Temasek.

- **MAS** holds Official Foreign Reserves for the exchange-rate regime. Most conservative, most liquid.
- **GIC** manages the long-duration government assets.
- **Temasek** is the commercial equity owner.

MOF, not the press, assigns capital among the three and reviews the *combined* risk. The elected President sits over Past Reserves and Fifth Schedule entities. NIR spending is a formula on expected long-term real returns; it is not a reason for GIC or Temasek to sell assets to feed the budget.

## Why a counterparty should care

If the ticket is a co-invest, a GP stake, or a control process in Asia, ask which legal person is across the table.

**Temasek** can hold a concentrated listed or unlisted stake, vote it, and sit through a restructuring. FY2026 examples in its own release: ST Telemedia’s remaining 82% of STT GDC sold to KKR and Singtel for S$6.6 billion; new money into Anthropic and OpenAI; an exit from Schneider Electric India. AI-related exposure was 6% of portfolio, with a stated aim of up to 15% by 2030.

**GIC** is the larger, quieter, more diversified book. It rarely wants control. It is a natural LP and real-assets partner. The 2026 framework language — granularity, agility, value creation on the way out — is how a reserve manager talks when it wants more active overlay without abandoning the 20-year real test.

They are not two share classes of the same fund. They do not share a portfolio. Treating them as synonyms is how a banker mis-drafts a teaser.

## Common mistakes

**“They compete.”** Complementary mandates. MAS is the third pool.

**“GIC is a classic commodity SWF.”** It is a surplus-reserves manager. It is grouped with SWFs because it is state-owned, long-horizon, and holds foreign financial assets — the Santiago tests — not because it banks an oil cheque.

**“Temasek is a passive holding company.”** Half the book is unlisted or funds; a quarter of the whole portfolio is listed stakes above 20%. That is an owner, not an index.

**“Add S$518 billion to ~US$900 billion.”** One number is Temasek’s. The other is an outsider’s guess at GIC. MOF does not publish a combined AUM.
